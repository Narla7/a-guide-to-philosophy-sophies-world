# Fichte

*Context: German Romanticism / post-Kantian idealism. Romantics saw themselves as Kant's successors: Kant established limit what can know of das Ding an sich, underlined ego's contribution to cognition. Individual now completely free to interpret life own way. Romantics exploited in almost unrestrained **ego-worship**, leading to exaltation of artistic genius.*

## 1. Ego

- Sum-up philosophers: viewed world soul as ego which in more/less dreamlike state created everything.
- **Fichte** said nature stems from higher, unconscious imagination.
- Descartes and Hume had drawn sharp line between ego and extended reality. Kant left sharp distinction between cognitive I and nature in itself. Romantic answer (via [[Schelling]]): **nature is nothing but one big I** — world soul / world spirit.

## 2. Unconscious imagination

- Nature stems from higher, unconscious imagination (Fichte's formula in source).
- Creative act not always completely conscious. Writer could experience story written by innate force. Practically hypnotic trance while wrote.
- Relationship artist/work same light. Fairy tale gave writer free rein to exploit universe-creating imagination.

## 3. Romantic irony (context from same source section)

- Then suddenly destroy illusion. Intervene story address ironic comments to reader, so reader momentarily reminded after all only story.
- At same time remind reader he manipulating fictional universe. This disillusion called **romantic irony**. Example Henrik Ibsen lets character in Peer Gynt say: *One cannot die in the middle of Act Five* — funny because saying only fictional character.
- Gaarder uses romantic irony self-referentially (Alberto addressing Hilde/major) but philosophy point: author breaks illusion to reveal fictionality, echoing world-soul dreaming.
- [[Schelling]] parallel in same section: world is in God; God aware some of it, other aspects unknown; **God also has dark side**.
