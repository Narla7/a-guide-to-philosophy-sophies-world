# Heraclitus
*Pre-Socratic natural philosopher — from Sophie's World, extreme-detail summary. Chapter context: first systematic philosophers ask — is there a basic substance everything is made of, and how can it change? Contemporary and opposite of Parmenides: reason vs flux, permanence vs becoming.*

The natural philosophers were the earliest Greek philosophers, each with his own "project" — what he was especially concerned to find out. They assumed "something" had always existed and asked what basic substance lies at the root of all change, seeking underlying laws by studying nature itself rather than myths. Only fragments survive, mostly via Aristotle two centuries later, who reports conclusions more than paths; what matters is the questions they asked and the type of answer they sought.

## Core thesis
Constant change / **flow** is the most basic characteristic of nature. **"Everything flows."** Heraclitus of Ephesus, Asia Minor (c. 540–480 B.C.) — contemporary of Parmenides.

## Arguments / reasoning (full detail, all examples from book)
- **River:** we "cannot step twice into the same river" — the second time, neither I nor the river are the same.
- **Opposites as constitutive:** if we were never ill, we would not know what it is to be well; without hunger, no pleasure in being full; without war, no appreciating peace; without winter, no spring. Good and bad each have an **inevitable place** in the order of things; without the constant interplay of opposites the world would cease to be.
- **God/logos:** "God is day and night, winter and summer, war and peace, hunger and satiety" — not mythological gods but that which embraces the whole world, seen in nature's transformations and contrasts. He often calls it **logos** (reason): a **universal reason/law** guiding all natural happenings, common to all, though most people live by their private reason. Contempt for the many: "The opinions of most people are like the playthings of infants."
- Amid flux and opposites there is an Entity — a one-ness — as the source of everything: God or logos.

## Key terms
"Everything flows" / river; opposites / unity-in-opposites; logos / universal reason; reliance on senses (change is real).

## Significance / legacy
First philosophy of becoming, unity-in-opposites, and universal law; the pole opposite Parmenides (reason vs senses, permanence vs flux) that Empedocles and Democritus must reconcile.

## Quotes
- "Everything flows" / "cannot step twice into the same river" (Heraclitus).
- "God is day and night, winter and summer, war and peace, hunger and satiety" (Heraclitus).
