# 23 - Darwin - Variation Struggle and Selection

*Context: Gaarder presents Darwin (1809–1882) as the naturalist who removed God from creation of species — evolution by natural selection, man's animal descent, and modern genetics/origins-of-life as extension.*

## 1. Naturalistic current and placement

- Marx and Darwin paths crossed in time/space: Marx in London 1849–1883, Darwin just outside London, died 1882 buried with pomp in Westminster Abbey. Marx wanted to dedicate English *Capital* to Darwin; Darwin declined.
- Engels: As Darwin discovered theory of organic evolution, so Marx discovered theory of mankind's historical evolution.
- Another linker: psychologist Sigmund Freud (last years in London). Freud said Darwin's evolution and his own psychoanalysis resulted in affront to mankind's naive egoism.
- Broader naturalistic current mid-19th century far into own time. "Naturalistic" = sense of reality accepting no other reality than nature/sensory world. Mankind part of nature. Scientist relies exclusively on natural phenomena — not rationalistic suppositions or divine revelation.
- Key words: nature, environment, history, evolution, growth. Marx: ideologies product of base. Darwin: mankind result of slow biological evolution. Freud: actions often result of animal urges/instincts.
- Pre-Socratics sought natural explanations vs myth; Darwin had to distance himself from church's view of creation as they did from myth.
- Darwin biologist/natural scientist, but also scientist of recent times who most openly challenged Biblical view of man's place in Creation.
- Noah with Ark board as ironic opener; Alberto turns board over: other side tree diagram of species distribution.

## 2. Life: Shrewsbury to Beagle

- Born Shrewsbury 1809. Father Dr. Robert Darwin renowned local physician, strict.
- Grammar school headmaster described him as boy always flying around, fooling with stuff and nonsense, never doing stroke of anything useful. "Useful" = cramming Greek/Latin verbs. "Flying around" = clambering collecting beetles of all kinds.
- Studied theology, far more interested in bird-watching/collecting insects, not very good grades. Still gained reputation as natural scientist, not least geology — most expansive science of day.
- Graduated theology Cambridge April 1831, went to North Wales study rock formations/search fossils. August same year, barely 22, letter determining whole life from friend/teacher John Steven Henslow:
> "I have been requested to ... recommend a naturalist to go as companion to Captain Fitzroy, who has been commissioned by the government to survey the southern coasts of South America. I have stated that I consider you to be the best qualified person I know of who is likely to undertake such a situation. As far as the financial side of it is concerned, I have no notion. The voyage is to last two years ..."

- Wished ardently, needed parents' consent, father finally agreed and financed. Financial side conspicuous by absence.
- Ship HMS Beagle sailed Plymouth Dec 27 1831 bound South America, did not return until Oct 1836. Two years became five; voyage to South America became voyage round world — via Pacific to New Zealand, Australia, South Africa, back to South America, England. Darwin: without doubt most significant event in life.
- First years up/down South American coast, plenty inland; forays into Galapagos west of South America decisive. Collected/sent vast material to England. Kept reflections to self. Returned age 27 renowned scientist, inwardly clear picture of evolution theory. Did not publish main work until many years after — cautious man, as fitting scientist.

## 3. *Origin* — two theses and full title as résumé

Main debated book: *The Origin of Species* (1859). Full title: *On the Origin of Species by Means of Natural Selection, or the Preservation of Favoured Races in the Struggle for Life* — actually complete résumé.

Gaarder stresses two theories:

1. All existing vegetable/animal forms descended from earlier more primitive forms by biological evolution.
2. Evolution result of natural selection.

First concentrate on idea of evolution itself — not all that original. Widely accepted in some circles as early as 1800. Leading spokesman French zoologist Lamarck. Before him Darwin's own grandfather Erasmus Darwin suggested plants/animals evolved from few primitive species. None had acceptable explanation how — therefore not considered great threat by churchmen. Darwin was, with reason.

Orthodoxy opposed:

- Ecclesiastic/scientific circles strictly adhered to Biblical doctrine of immutability of species. Every animal form created separately once and for all.
- In harmony with Plato (species immutable because made after eternal ideas/forms) and Aristotle (immutability cornerstone).
- In Darwin's time observations/finds putting beliefs to test.

## 4. Evidence for evolution: fossils, Lyell, geography, embryos

### 4.1 Fossils and catastrophe vs gradualism

- Increasing fossils dug out, large fossil bones extinct animals. Darwin puzzled to find traces sea creatures far inland, in South America high in Andes. What is sea creature doing in Andes?
- Some believed thrown away by humans/animals. Others God created fossils to lead ungodly astray.
- Most geologists swore to "catastrophe theory": earth subjected to gigantic floods, earthquakes, catastrophes destroying all life (Bible Flood/Noah's Ark one). After each, God renewed life by creating new — more perfect — plants/animals. Fossils imprints of earlier life wiped out, e.g. animals failed to get into Ark.
- Darwin had vol.1 of Charles Lyell's *Principles of Geology* on Beagle. Lyell: present geology — mountains/valleys — result of interminably long gradual evolution. Even small changes cause huge upheavals given aeons: same forces prevailing today — wind/weather, melting ice, earthquakes, elevations. Drop of water wearing away stone not by brute force but continuous dripping. Similar tiny gradual changes alter face of nature completely.
- Alone could not explain sea remains in Andes, but Darwin remembered: tiny gradual changes + sufficient time = dramatic alterations. Thought same could apply to animals.
- Cautious method: posed questions long before venturing answers — same method as true philosophers: important to ask, no haste to answer.

### 4.2 Age of earth

- Decisive factor: in Darwin's time widely believed ~6,000 years since creation, counting generations since Adam and Eve.
- Darwin figured 300 million years. At least clear: neither Lyell gradualism nor Darwin evolution valid unless tremendously long periods.
- Today know 4.6 billion years.

### 4.3 Geographic distribution and Galapagos

Second argument: distribution of living species — Beagle's comprehensive data. Seen with own eyes individuals of single species within same region could differ in minutest detail.

- Galapagos compact volcanic islands, no great differences in flora/fauna — therefore tiny differences interesting.
- Giant tortoises slightly different island to island. Had God created separate race for each island?
- Finches even more striking: clearly varied island to island, especially beak shape. Demonstrated variations closely linked to food-finding: ground finches steeply profiled beaks lived on pine cone seeds, little warbler finches on insects, tree finches on termites from bark/branches. Each beak perfectly adapted to own intake. Descended from same species? Adapted over ages so new species evolved?
- Yes — maybe there Darwin became "Darwinist."
- Fauna strongly resembled South American species. Had God created all slightly different, or had evolution taken place? Increasingly doubted immutability. Still no viable mechanism.

### 4.4 Embryology

One more factor all animals related: mammalian embryo development. Compare embryos dogs, bats, rabbits, humans at early stage — look so alike hard to tell difference. Cannot distinguish human from rabbit until very late. Indicates distant relatives.

## 5. Mechanism hunt: Lamarck rejected, artificial selection as clue

- Pondered Lyell's minute changes with great effect, but no general principle.
- Familiar with Lamarck: species developed characteristics needed. Giraffes developed long necks because for generations reached up for leaves. Acquired characteristics passed to next generation. Rejected by Darwin — no proof of bold claims.
- Much more obvious line right in front of nose: artificial selection.

Socratic dialogue Gaarder reconstructs:

- Three cows, fodder for two — slaughter one: the least milky. Logical.
- Which to calve? Best milker — calf probably good milker.
- Two gundogs, give up one — keep best at finding game shot.
- Exactly how people bred domestic animals >10,000 years. Hens did not always lay five eggs/week, sheep not always so much wool, horses not always strong/swift. Breeders made artificial selection. Same vegetable kingdom: don't plant bad potatoes if good seed available, don't cut wheat yielding no grain.
- Darwin point: no cows, wheat, dogs, finches completely alike. Nature produces enormous breadth of variation. Even same species, no two exactly alike — Sophie experienced when drank blue liquid.
- Question: could similar mechanism work in nature? Could nature make "natural selection" as to which survive? Could such selection over long time create new species? Guess yes.

But how does natural selection take place? Oct 1838, two years after return, chanced on Thomas Malthus *An Essay on the Principle of Population*.

- Malthus got idea from Benjamin Franklin (lightning conductor inventor): if no limiting factors, single species would spread over entire globe. Because many species, keep each other in balance.
- Malthus applied to population: mankind's procreation so great always more children born than can survive. Food production never keeps pace with increase, huge numbers destined to succumb in struggle for existence. Survivors perpetuating race those coming out best.
- This universal mechanism Darwin searched for. Explanation how evolution happens: natural selection in struggle for life, best adapted survive and perpetuate race. Second theory in *Origin*.
- Darwin's elephant arithmetic: elephant slowest breeder, but if six young survived to 100, after 740–750 years nearly nineteen million elephants alive descended from first pair. Not to mention thousands of cod's eggs from single cod.

Refinements:

- Struggle frequently hardest among most resembling species — fight for same food. Slightest advantage — infinitesimal variation — truly comes into own. More bitter struggle, quicker evolution of new species, only best adapted survive, others die out. Less food + bigger brood = quicker evolution.
- Not only food: avoid being eaten — protective camouflage, swift running, recognize hostiles, repellent taste, killer poison. Many cacti poisonous — practically nothing else grows in desert, plant especially vulnerable.
- Reproduction fundamental: Darwin studied plant pollination ingenuity — flowers glow hues/exude scents to attract insects; birds trill; placid/melancholy bull with no interest in cows no interest for genealogy — line dies. Bull's sole purpose grow to maturity and reproduce to propagate race — like relay race. Unable to pass genes continually discarded, race continually refined. Disease resistance most important progressively accumulated/preserved.
- Result: ones best adapted to particular environment/ecological niche perpetuate race there. Advantage in one environment not necessarily in another. Some Galapagos finches flying vital; not so necessary if food dug from ground and no predators. Many niches explain many species arisen.
- Man single race because unique ability to adapt. Darwin amazed how Tierra del Fuego Indians lived under terrible climate. Doesn't mean alike: equator darker skins protect from sun; whites exposing long prone to skin cancer. White advantage in north? Yes otherwise all dark — white more easily forms sun vitamins vital where little sun. Nowadays diet compensates. Nothing random — infinitesimal changes over countless generations.

Summary formula Gaarder gives:

> "We can say that the 'raw material' behind the evolution of life on earth was the continual variation of individuals within the same species, plus the large number of progeny, which meant that only a fraction of them survived, the actual 'mechanism,' or driving force, behind evolution was thus the natural selection in the struggle for survival. This selection ensured that the strongest, or the 'fittest,' survived."

"It seems as logical as a math sum."

## 6. Reception and Descent of Man

- Bitter controversies. Church protested vehemently, scientific world sharply divided. Not surprising — distanced God good way from creation, though some claimed greater to have created something with innate evolutionary potential than fixed entity. Adam/Eve appear as fairy-tale figures with Red Ridinghood/Alice.
- 1871 *The Descent of Man*: great similarities humans/animals, men and anthropoid apes must once evolved from same progenitor. By then first fossil skulls extinct man found — Rock of Gibraltar, years later Neanderthal Germany. Strangely fewer protests 1871 than 1859, but descent implicit in first book. When died 1882 buried with ceremony due pioneer.
- Eventually honor/dignity, but first described as most dangerous man in England.
- Upper-class lady: "Let us hope it is not true, but if it is, let us hope it will not be generally known." Scientist: "An embarrassing discovery, and the less said about it the better." Gaarder jokes almost proof man related to ostrich.
- People obliged to revise Genesis. John Ruskin: "If only the geologists would leave me alone. After each Bible verse I hear the blows of their hammers." Hammers = doubts about word of God.
- More than literal creation toppled. Essence: utterly random variations finally produced Man. Moreover Darwin turned Man into product of unsentimental struggle for existence.

## 7. Weak point: heredity → neo-Darwinism, mutations

- Did Darwin say how random variations arose? Vaguest idea of heredity. Something happens in crossing. Father/mother never get two identical offspring — always slight difference. Difficult to produce really new that way. Moreover plants/animals reproduce by budding/simple cell division. Supplemented by neo-Darwinism.
- All life/reproduction basically cell division. Cell divides into two identical with exactly same hereditary factors — copies itself.
- Occasionally infinitesimal errors — copied cell not exactly same as mother. In modern terms mutation. Either totally irrelevant, or marked behavior changes. Can be directly harmful — mutants continually discarded from large broods. Many diseases due to mutations. Sometimes gives just extra positive characteristic to hold own in struggle.
- Giraffe example revisited:
  - Lamarck: always had to reach upwards, acquired long neck passed on.
  - Darwin: no such inherited characteristic passed on; long neck result of variation.
  - Neo-Darwinism shows clear cause: absolutely random changes in hereditary factors supplied one ancestor slightly longer neck than average. When limited food, vital. Highest reacher managed best. Also imagine primal giraffes evolving ability to dig ground for food. Over long time, long-extinct species could divide into two species.

## 8. Modern textbook examples Gaarder details

### Peppered moth (called butterfly in translation)

- Species lives on silver birch trunks. 18th century most silvery gray — so not easy for hungry birds to spot.
- From time to time chance mutations some darker born. Fared? Easier seen, more easily snapped up. In silver environment darker unfavorable. Always paler increased.
- Then environment changed: several places trunks blackened by industrial soot. Darker survived best. 1848–1948 proportion dark increased 1 → 99% in certain places. White losers weeded out by birds.
- Then significant again: decrease coal use + better filtering → cleaner environment. Birches silver again. Moth returning to silvery. This is adaptation. Natural law.

### Pesticides / antibiotics — man interferes

- Spray field/orchard — miniature ecocatastrophe for pests. Excellent at first. Due continual mutations, resistant type develops, winners have free play, harder to combat because of attempt to eradicate. Most resistant survive.
- Same own bodies bacteria: penicillin ecocatastrophe for little devils. Continue administering, make certain bacteria resistant, cultivating much harder group, need stronger antibiotics, until... (Sophie jokes finally crawl out mouths, shoot them).
- Modern medicine serious dilemma: not only single bacterium more virulent. In past many children never survived — succumbed diseases, sometimes minority survived. Modern medicine put natural selection out of commission. Something helping one individual over serious illness can long run weaken resistance of whole race. If pay no attention to hereditary hygiene, could face degeneration — hereditary potential resisting serious disease weakened.
- Real philosopher must not refrain pointing out "terrifying" if believes true.

Lottery summary:

> "You could say that life is one big lottery in which only the winning numbers are visible."

Those lost disappeared. Takes many millions years to select winning numbers for each species. Losing numbers only one appearance. No species today not winning numbers in great lottery. Because only best survived.

## 9. Tree of life, primal cell, hot little pool

- Noah board other side: tree diagram distribution — species belong in groups/classes/subkingdoms. Monkeys + man = primates; primates mammals; mammals vertebrates; vertebrates multicellular. Almost like Aristotle. But also history: birds parted reptiles, reptiles amphibia, amphibia fishes.
- Every line dividing into two because mutations resulted in new species. How classes/subkingdoms arose. >1 million animal species today, only fraction ever lived. Trilobita totally extinct.
- Bottom monocellular animals — some unchanged two billion years. Line from monocellular to vegetable kingdom — plants probably same primal cell as animals.
- Where first primal cell from? Cautious man permitted qualified guess:
> "If (and O, what an if!) we could picture some hot little pool in which all manner of ammoniacal and phosphorous salts, light, heat, electricity and so forth were present, and that a protein compound were to be chemically formed in it, ready to undergo even more complicated changes ..."

Gaarder: philosophizing how first living cell formed from inorganic matter — hit nail. Scientists today think first primitive life arose in precisely such hot little pool.

## 10. Origins of life — Gaarder's update beyond Darwin

- Will jump to most recent findings. Maybe not know how began, but more pieces forming picture.
- All life animal/vegetable constructed of exactly same substances. Simplest definition: substance which in nutrient solution has ability to subdivide into two identical parts. Governed by DNA — chromosomes/hereditary structures in all living cells, complex molecule/macromolecule. Question how first molecule arose.
- Earth formed solar system 4.6 bn years ago as glowing mass gradually cooled. Modern science believes life began 3–4 bn years ago. Sounds improbable — hear rest.
- Planet quite different: no life, no oxygen in atmosphere. Free oxygen first formed by photosynthesis. Important: life cells which can form DNA unlikely arisen in oxygen atmosphere. Oxygen strongly reactive — long before complex DNA molecules formed, cells would be oxidized.
- Hence know for certain no new life arises today, not even bacterium/virus. All life exactly same age. Elephant just as long family tree as smallest bacterium. Almost say elephant/human in reality single coherent colony of monocellular creatures. Each cell carries same hereditary material. Whole recipe hidden in each tiny cell.
- Mystery: multicellular cells specialize despite not all hereditary characteristics active in all cells. Some genes activated, others deactivated. Liver cell does not produce same proteins as nerve/brain/skin, but all have same DNA containing whole recipe.
- No oxygen → no protective ozone → nothing to stop cosmic radiation. Significant: radiation probably instrumental forming first complex molecule. Actual energy causing chemical substances to combine into macromolecule.
- Recapitulation: before complex molecules of which all life consists, at least two conditions: no oxygen, access cosmic radiation.
- In "hot little pool" — primal soup — once formed gigantically complicated macromolecule with wondrous property subdividing into two identical parts. Long evolution began. Simplify: first hereditary material, first DNA/living cell. Subdivided again and again — but from first stage transmutation occurring. After aeons one monocellular connected with more complicated multicellular. Photosynthesis began, atmosphere oxygenated. Two results: atmosphere permitted lung-breathing animals; protected from harmful cosmic rays. Strangely radiation vital spark also harmful.
- Life began in primal seas/soup protected. Much later, when oceans formed atmosphere, first amphibians crawled land. Rest already talked. Here sitting in hut looking back 3–4 bn years. In us long process finally become aware of itself.

## 11. Meaning? Eye, Faust, living planet

- Picture shows evolution had direction: across aeons animals evolved increasingly complicated nerve systems, ever bigger brain. Personally [Alberto] don't think accidental.
- Sophie: can't be pure chance created human eye. Meaning in being able to see world? Development of eye puzzled Darwin too — couldn't really come to terms something delicate/sensitive exclusively due to natural selection.
- Sophie quotes Mephistopheles/Devil from Goethe's *Faust* (note she found in Alberto's brother letter):
> "What matters our creative endless toil, When at a snatch, oblivion ends the coil?"

- Alberto: must not talk like that — words of Devil. Faust dying looks back triumph:
> "Then to the moment could I say: Linger you now, you are so fair! Now records of my earthly day No flights of aeons can impair--Foreknowledge comes, and fills me with such bliss, I take my joy, my highest moment this."

- As soon as Faust dies Devil exclaims:
> "A foolish word, bygone. How so then, gone? Gone, to sheer Nothing, past with null made one! What matters creative endless toil, When, at a snatch, oblivion ends the coil? 'It is bygone'--How shall this riddle run? As good as if things never had begun, Yet circle back, existence to possess: I'd rather have Eternal Emptiness."

- Sophie liked first passage — even though life over, Faust saw meaning in traces leave behind.
- Alberto's Darwinian consequence: we are part of something all-encompassing, every tiny life form significance in big picture.
> "We are the living planet, Sophie! We are the great vessel sailing around a burning sun in the universe. But each and every one of us is also a ship sailing through life with a cargo of genes. When we have carried this cargo safely to the next harbor--we have not lived in vain."

- Same thought Thomas Hardy *Transformations*: yew portion man grandsire knew, branch may be wife ruddy life turned green shoot, grasses her who prayed, fair girl entering rose — not underground, but as nerves/veins in upper air, feel sun/rain, energy again that made them what they were.
