# Link Hub - Middle Ages

*Context: Hub for the Christian Middle Ages (~400–1400) — faith vs. reason, three cultures, and the road from Augustine to Aquinas.*

## 1. Persons

- [[Augustine]] — Early Middle Ages (354–430); christianized Plato; illumination; Ideas in God's mind; evil as privation; City of God; predestination / foreknowledge.
- [[Aquinas]] — High Middle Ages (1225–1274); christianized Aristotle; one truth two paths; natural theology (Five Ways not enumerated in Gaarder's passage here); scale of being; natural morality; women critique.
- [[Hildegard of Bingen]] — (1098–1179); nun, preacher, physician, botanist; Sophia vision (golden tunic with jewels); female Wisdom side of God.

## 2. Historical Frame (preserved in full from source chapter)

### 2.1 Dating and the "clock" analogy
- Antiquity ends; Christian Middle Ages lasts ~1,000 years.
- Alberto's conceit: Jesus born at midnight; each hour = 100 years.
  - Paul missions ~00:30; dies Rome ~00:45.
  - Christianity banned ~03:00; accepted 313 under Constantine; official religion 380.
  - 04:00 = ~400, start of Middle Ages; High Gothic ~11:00–13:00 (great cathedrals); cock crows ~14:00 (~1400), Middle Ages ebbs.
- "Renaissance" coined the pejorative "Middle Ages" / "Dark Ages": a thousand-year night between antiquity and rebirth. Gaarder notes modern historians rehabilitate it as germination and growth.
- Story hook: Sophie meets Alberto disguised as a monk in St. Mary's Church.

### 2.2 Fall of Rome and Christian state power
- Rome threatened by northern barbarians plus internal disintegration.
- 330: Constantine moves capital Rome → Constantinople ("second Rome" / later Byzantium / Istanbul).
- 395: Empire split Western (Rome) / Eastern (Constantinople).
- 410: Rome plundered; 476: Western Empire destroyed; Eastern Empire survives until Turkish conquest of Constantinople, 1453.
- Politically Rome falls by late 4th century, but Bishop of Rome becomes Pope (*papa*), Christ's deputy; Rome remains Christian capital through most of the period. Later kings / bishops of nation-states challenge church power — the medieval Church–State struggle.
- Norway's case: Christianized only in 11th century; heathen customs persist under Christianity (e.g. Yule cookies / piglets / ale fused with Three Wise Men / manger). Christianity nevertheless becomes predominant life-philosophy: a unifying Christian culture.

### 2.3 Early medieval decline and feudal order
- First centuries after 400: genuine cultural decline from Roman high culture (cities, sewers, baths, libraries, architecture).
- Trade / economy crumble; return to payment in kind and barter; feudalism: few nobles own land, serfs toil to live.
- Population collapse: Rome >1,000,000 in antiquity → 40,000 by 600; survivors quarry ruins for building stone, to modern archaeologists' sorrow.

### 2.4 Institutions: the preservation of learning
- 529 as symbol: Church closes Plato's Academy in Athens; Benedictine order (first great monastic order) founded. Monasteries gain monopoly on education, reflection, meditation.
- Convent schools early; cathedral schools in 12th century; first universities ~1200 with "faculties" as today.
- School system itself is a medieval creation.
- Nation-states, cities / citizens, folk music / folktales, fairy tales develop: England, France, Germany carry medieval resonance. Figures named: Snorri, St. Olaf, Charlemagne, Romeo and Juliet, Joan of Arc, Ivanhoe, Pied Piper, knights / damsels, glass-makers, organ-builders, friars, crusaders, witches.

### 2.5 The three streams and the river metaphor
- Old Empire divides into three cultures:
  - West: Latinized Roman Catholic, capital Rome.
  - East: Greek Christian / Byzantine, capital Constantinople / Byzantium.
  - South: Arabic-speaking Muslim (Middle East, North Africa, then Spain after Muhammad's death 632; holy cities Mecca, Medina, Jerusalem, Baghdad; inherits Hellenistic Alexandria).
- Arabs preserve Greek science and lead in mathematics, chemistry, astronomy, medicine; "Arabic figures" still used; in several areas superior to Christian culture.
- Transmission, oversimplified: Neoplatonism handed down in the West, Plato in the East, Aristotle to the Arabs in the South — with admixture in each stream.
- Image: one broad river divides into three streams, then reunites in Northern Italy at the end of the Middle Ages (Arabic influence via Spain, Greek via Byzantium / Greece) → Renaissance. Antique culture thus survived the Dark Ages.

### 2.6 The central medieval philosophical question
- Medieval philosophers take Christianity's truth almost for granted.
- Question: must we simply believe revelation, or can reason approach Christian truths? Relation of Greek philosophers to Bible? Contradiction between Bible and reason, or compatibility of belief and knowledge?
- Almost all medieval philosophy centers on this.

## 3. Faith vs. Reason Table

|  | Augustine (Plato christianized) | Aquinas (Aristotle christianized) |
|---|---|---|
| **Relation faith–reason** | Reason limited; faith needed; faith lets God illuminate soul to supernatural knowledge | No necessary conflict; one truth, two paths; going part-way ≠ going wrong way (Athens/Europe analogy) |
| **Path(s) to God** | Philosophy part-way; peace only through faith: "Our heart is not quiet until it rests in Thee" | Faith + revelation (surest) AND reason + senses; overlap; natural theological truths (that God is) vs. verities of faith (six days, Jesus as Son) |
| **Use of Greek** | Ideas relocated into Divine mind; creation *ex nihilo* keeps eternity within Christianity | Formal cause; logic, knowledge, nature adopted where not colliding; scale of being plants→animals→man→angels→God; timeless divine vision |
| **Evil / Morals** | Evil = privation (*privatio boni*), absence of God; disobedience; good will vs. falling away | Bible + natural conscience ("do unto others" known naturally); revealed + natural morality |
| **History / Church** | City/Kingdom of God vs. City/World; Church as Kingdom present; linear history Adam→end as one man childhood→old age | Aristotle newly reclaimed via Arabs; Church synthesis at noon (~1200s) |
| **Gender note** | — | Adopts Aristotle: woman incomplete/passive (rib); soul equal, Heaven equality; Gaarder "Nonsense!" (ova 1827); counter-voice [[Hildegard of Bingen]] + Sophia |

## 4. Medieval Worldview in One Paragraph (preserved)

- Theocentric starting point (God, not man); revelation above reason but reason not void; nature studied yet barrier between Creator and Creation; history linear and purposive toward Kingdom; Church as ark of salvation; Aristotle newly reclaimed via Arabs, Plato via Fathers — the "three streams" about to rejoin.
