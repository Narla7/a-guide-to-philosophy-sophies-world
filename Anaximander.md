# Anaximander
*Pre-Socratic natural philosopher — from Sophie's World, extreme-detail summary. Chapter context: first systematic philosophers ask — is there a basic substance everything is made of, and how can it change? (Water into wine? Earth + water into a live frog?)*

The natural philosophers were the earliest Greek philosophers, each with his own "project" — what he was especially concerned to find out. They assumed "something" had always existed and asked what basic substance lies at the root of all change — how live fish come from water, trees from dead earth, a baby from a womb — seeking underlying laws by studying nature itself rather than myths. Only fragments survive, mostly via Aristotle two centuries later, who reports conclusions more than paths; what matters is the questions they asked and the type of answer they sought.

## Core thesis
Our world is one of a **myriad of worlds** evolving and dissolving in **the boundless** (apeiron). Anaximander of Miletus, contemporary of Thales.

## Arguments / reasoning (full detail, all examples from book)
- The source cannot be a known, limited substance like water.
- All created things are limited; what comes before and after them must be "boundless" — something **other than the things created**.
- Hence the basic stuff cannot be anything as ordinary as water: Thales' answer is too concrete, too finite, to ground everything that comes to be and passes away.

## Key terms
The boundless (apeiron); plurality of worlds; indefinite principle vs observable element.

## Significance / legacy
First abstraction beyond an observable element to an indefinite principle; advances the Milesian single-substance project while criticizing Thales' choice of water.

## Quotes
- "Is there a basic substance that everything else is made of?"
