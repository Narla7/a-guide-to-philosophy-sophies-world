# Thales
*Pre-Socratic natural philosopher — from Sophie's World, extreme-detail summary. Chapter context: first systematic philosophers ask — is there a basic substance everything is made of, and how can it change? (Water into wine? Earth + water into a live frog?)*

The natural philosophers were the earliest Greek philosophers, each with his own "project" — what he was especially concerned to find out (plants, gods, the soul). They assumed "something" had always existed and asked what basic substance lies at the root of all change — how live fish come from water, trees and flowers from dead earth, a baby from a womb. Only fragments survive, mostly via Aristotle two centuries later, who reports conclusions more than the paths of reasoning; what matters most is which questions they asked and what type of answer they sought — laws found by studying nature itself, liberating philosophy from religion.

## Core thesis
The source of all things is **water**. Thales of Miletus (Asia Minor; predicted the solar eclipse of 585 B.C.; measured the pyramid by its shadow at the moment his own shadow equaled his height; traveled in Egypt) posited the first natural, non-mythical arché.

## Arguments / reasoning (full detail, all examples from book)
- In Egypt he saw crops grow once the Nile floods receded from the Delta; frogs and worms appear where it has just rained — life seeming to come from water.
- Water visibly changes form: water ↔ ice ↔ vapor and back — a directly observable model of transformation.
- Conjectured: all life originates from water and returns to water on dissolving.
- **"All things are full of gods":** not Homer's gods; the surmised meaning is that the black earth as source of flowers, crops, insects, cockroaches suggests the earth is filled with tiny invisible **"life-germs."**

## Key terms
arché as water; transformation via observed states (liquid/ice/vapor); "life-germs"; Nile/frogs observation.

## Significance / legacy
First philosopher known; first natural (non-mythical) arché; sets the Milesian project of a single basic substance that Anaximander and Anaximenes will revise.

## Quotes
- "All things are full of gods" (Thales).
- "Is there a basic substance that everything else is made of?"; "Can water turn into wine? How can earth and water produce a live frog!"
