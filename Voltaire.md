# Voltaire

*Context: French Enlightenment (mid-18th century). After Hume, Alberto gives Sophie the French Enlightenment as seven-point program. Philosophical center of gravity in 18th century: **England first half, France middle, Germany toward end** — shift west to east. Important French names: **Montesquieu, Voltaire, and Rousseau**, plus many others.*

## 1. Life anchors (from source only)

- Major figure of the French Enlightenment alongside [[Montesquieu]] and [[Rousseau]].
- Contemporary of Hume: Hume died 1776, only 2 years before Voltaire and Rousseau (both 1778).
- Like Montesquieu and Rousseau, had been to England, knew Locke.
- No separate satire / Candide passage inside the source chunk (lines 11800–14600). Do not invent one from outside — source concentrates not on separate biographies but on **seven common points**.

## 2. Doctrines shared (the seven-point program he belongs to)

1. Opposition to authority
2. Rationalism
3. The enlightenment movement
4. Cultural optimism
5. The return to nature
6. Natural religion
7. Human rights

- Slogan later hung in cabin: **LIBERTY, EQUALITY AND FRATERNITY**. In-story letter links it to UN ideals: 200 years ago united France, today should unite world as one Family of Man; descendants/children inherit world.
- Revolutionary ideas arose much earlier than **French Revolution 1789**.

### Point 1 — Opposition to authority

- Many French philosophers visited England, more liberal than home, intrigued by English natural science, especially **Newton and universal physics**, and British philosophy, especially Locke's political philosophy.
- Back in France, increasingly opposed old authority; essential to remain skeptical of inherited truths; individual must find own answer to every question. Descartes tradition (building from ground) inspiring.
- Directed not least against power of **clergy, king, nobility** — far more powerful in 18th-century France than England.

### Point 2 — Rationalism / Age of Reason

- Hume hasn't killed rationalism: Locke himself inconsistent empiricist — believed faith in God and certain moral norms inherent in human reason. That idea is core of French Enlightenment.
- National difference noted: British "common sense" = what everybody knows; French "evident" = what is obvious to reason. French more rational since Middle Ages.
- Like humanists of antiquity (Socrates, Stoics), Enlightenment philosophers had unshakable faith in human reason — hence **Age of Reason**.
- New natural sciences revealed nature subject to reason; duty now to lay foundation for morals, religion, ethics in accordance with man's immutable reason.

### Points 3–7 (overview — full detail in hub)

- Point 3 — Enlightenment movement and Encyclopedia (28 volumes, 1751–1772, needles to cannons, pedagogy founded).
- Point 4 — Cultural optimism (reason/knowledge → progress; early criticism of "civilization" — "Maybe we should have listened").
- Point 5 — Back to nature: [[Rousseau]] core.
- Point 6 — Natural religion / Deism (Supreme Being, Aristotle parallel).
- Point 7 — Human rights, slavery, press, law (Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen 1789, basis for Norway's Constitution 1814). Women's-rights struggle in [[Olympe de Gouges]].
