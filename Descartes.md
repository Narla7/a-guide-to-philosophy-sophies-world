# Descartes

*Context: René Descartes (1596–1650) — father of modern philosophy, first great system-builder after the Renaissance, seeking certain knowledge by methodic doubt, cogito, God, and dualism.*

## 1. Life

**Thesis:** Like Socrates, Descartes finds inherited learning unreliable and seeks wisdom in himself and "the great book of the world."

**Arguments / Details:**
- Born 1596, lived in many European countries; early desire for insight into man / universe; after study, convinced of own ignorance (like Socrates).
- Rationalist lineage: Socrates–Plato–Augustine–Descartes: certain knowledge only through reason; distrust old books and senses (Plato: reason more certain than senses; mathematics more certain).
- Medieval learning unreliable; like Socrates distrusting Athens marketplace opinion, Descartes travels Europe – seeks wisdom "within himself or in the great book of the world"; joins army, spends time in Central Europe; Paris; Holland 1629–~20 years (math + philosophy); 1649 invited by Queen Christina to Sweden – "land of bears, ice, rocks" – pneumonia, dies winter 1650, age 54.
- Enormous influence: father of modern philosophy; first significant system-builder after Renaissance welter. System = philosophy built from ground up explaining all central questions (Plato / Aristotle in Antiquity; Aquinas bridging Aristotle + Christianity; then gap until 17th century: Descartes, then Spinoza, Leibniz, Locke, Berkeley, Hume, Kant).
- His two obsessions that dominate next 150 years: (1) what we can know / certain knowledge; (2) body–mind relation.

**Terms:** rationalism; system-builder; father of modern philosophy.

## 2. Method

**Thesis:** Philosophy needs a certain exact method like science's new method — mathematical, stepwise, gapless.

**Arguments / Details:**
- *Discourse on Method* (first in 1637 essays volume – Alberto treasures first edition).
- Science has new certain exact method; philosophy needs analogous certain exact method of reflection.
- Rules:
  1. Accept nothing as true unless clearly and distinctly perceived.
  2. Break compound problem into as many single factors as possible.
  3. Start from simplest idea, go simple → complex; weigh / measure every thought (Galileo: measure everything, make immeasurable measurable).
  4. By constant enumeration and control ensure nothing omitted.
- Mathematician: father of analytic geometry, algebra; wants mathematical method in philosophy – prove philosophical truths like theorems, using reason alone, since only reason gives certainty.

**Terms:** clear and distinct perception; analysis / synthesis; enumeration.

## 3. Methodic Doubt in Full Steps — Dream to Zero to Cogito

**Thesis:** Clear the site to zero by doubting everything, then build only from what reason sees intuitively.

**Arguments / Steps (full):**
- Aim: certainty about nature of life; must not build on sand or rubble – clear site, use only fresh materials, else house collapses.
- **Step 1 – reject authority:** do not advance by reading Plato / Aristotle – increases historical knowledge, not world knowledge; rid self of handed-down learning.
- **Step 2 – distrust senses:** senses may deceive.
- **Step 3 – dream argument:** "When I consider this carefully, I find not a single property which with certainty separates the waking state from the dream." We feel dreaming as reality. "How can you be certain that your whole life is not a dream?" Like Jeppe: in Baron's bed thinks peasant life dream; in ditch thinks Baron's bed dream. See also Baroque dream theater in [[Baroque - Era Overview]] — Shakespeare / Calderón / Chuang-tzu background.
- Result: doubt absolutely everything – zero point where many earlier philosophers stopped.
- Descartes works forward from zero: the one thing certain is that he doubts; doubting = thinking; thinking → must be thinking being: ***Cogito, ergo sum* – "I think, therefore I am."**
- Intuitive certainty; grasped by reason more real than material world grasped by senses (Plato). Thinking I more real than extended world.

**Terms:** methodic doubt; dream argument; zero point; *Cogito, ergo sum*.

**Quotes:** "When I consider this carefully, I find not a single property which with certainty separates the waking state from the dream." / "How can you be certain that your whole life is not a dream?" / "Cogito, ergo sum – I think, therefore I am."

## 4. God and Outer World

**Thesis:** From cogito, reason also sees God and mathematized outer reality with the same intuitive certainty, guaranteed against dream.

**Arguments:**
- Next: anything else perceived with same intuitive certainty? Yes: in mind a **clear and distinct idea of a perfect entity,** always had, self-evident.
  - Cannot have come from self, who is imperfect; must originate from perfect entity itself – God. Existence of God as self-evident as thinking being.
  - Not a proof but unpacking innate idea: perfection inherently includes existence, as circle inherently includes equidistance from center; you cannot have circle violating that, nor perfect entity lacking existence. Idea of God innate, stamped like artisan's mark.
  - Objection (Sophie): possessing idea of "crocophant" doesn't make it exist. Reply: "crocophant" does not contain existence; "perfect entity" does.
  - Rationalist link reason–being: more self-evident to reason, more certain to exist (Socrates / Plato).
- Outer reality: ideas of sun / moon may be fantasies, but outer reality has characteristics perceivable by reason: mathematical / measurable – length, breadth, depth – **quantitative** properties, clear / distinct as cogito. **Qualitative** – color, smell, taste – linked to senses, do not describe outer reality.
- **God's guarantee:** perfect God would not deceive; whatever reason perceives clearly / distinctly (mathematical properties) must correspond to reality. So nature is not dream after all.
- Achieved: (1) I am thinking being; (2) perfect entity (God) exists; (3) outer extended reality exists.

**Terms:** innate idea of perfection; existence as perfection; quantitative vs. qualitative; God's guarantee / non-deceiver.

## 5. Dualism in Detail — Res Cogitans / Res Extensa, Pineal Gland, Man-Machine

**Thesis:** Reality is two substances — thought and extension — both from God but without contact, making man a dual creature and body a machine.

**Arguments:**
- Two different substances (*substance* = what something consists of / reducible to):
  - ***res cogitans* – thought / mind:** purely conscious, takes no room in space, indivisible into parts.
  - ***res extensa* – extension / matter:** purely extended, takes room, always divisible smaller, no consciousness.
  - Both originate from God (only God exists independently), but have no contact; thought independent of matter, material processes independent of thought. Sharp division – **dualist.**
- Consequences:
  - Only man has mind; animals wholly extended reality, living / moving mechanically – complicated automata. Extended reality thoroughly mechanistic (like materialists). Human body also perfect machine (admiration of clocks / automata – *automaton* = self-mover, apparent self-motion really human construction / winding; from few parts vs. vast bones / muscles / nerves / veins – why shouldn't God make body mechanically?).
  - Man dual creature: mind + extended body (Augustine / Aquinas similarly: body like animals, soul like angels). Body obeys mechanical laws without freedom; mind operates independently of body.
  - Interaction problem: constant mind–body interaction undeniable (decide lift arm → arm lifts; run for bus → legs move; sad thought → cry). Solution: while mind in body, linked through **pineal gland,** special brain organ where spirit–matter interact. Mind affected by bodily passions / needs, but can detach from base impulses, let reason command. E.g. stomach pain doesn't change triangle angles sum 180°. Mind superior: legs age, back bows, teeth fall, but 2+2=4 as long as reason remains; reason doesn't bow. Mind = thought; baser passions (desire, hate) belong to body / extension.
  - Allows animals might think, but then same dualism applies to them.
- Modern echo: "artificial intelligence" as our automaton; machines deceiving us they are intelligent would terrify Descartes, force doubt whether reason so free; some philosophers hold soul as unfree as data program, though soul infinitely more complex.

**Terms:** *res cogitans* vs. *res extensa*; substance; dualism; pineal gland; automaton; man-machine; interaction problem.

**Legacy:** two obsessions dominate next 150 years — certainty and mind–body; answered by monism, empiricism, and critique.
