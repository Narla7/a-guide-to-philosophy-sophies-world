# Anaxagoras
*Pre-Socratic natural philosopher — from Sophie's World, extreme-detail summary. Chapter context: first systematic philosophers ask — is there a basic substance everything is made of, and how can it change? Pluralist answer after Empedocles: infinite seeds + cosmic Mind.*

The natural philosophers were the earliest Greek philosophers, each with his own "project" — what he was especially concerned to find out. They assumed "something" had always existed and asked what basic substance lies at the root of all change, seeking underlying laws by studying nature itself rather than myths. Only fragments survive, mostly via Aristotle two centuries later, who reports conclusions more than paths; what matters is the questions they asked and the type of answer they sought.

## Core thesis
Nature is built of **infinite minute invisible particles ("seeds")**; everything is divisible into smaller parts, yet even the minutest parts contain **fragments of all other things** — "something of everything in everything." Skin and bone cannot be a transformation of something else — so milk and food must already contain skin and bone. Anaxagoras (500–428 B.C.).

## Arguments / reasoning (full detail, all examples from book)
- **Modern illustrations the book itself uses:** **hologram** — a fragment showing only the bumper still shows the whole car, because the whole subject is present in each tiny part; **human cell** — a skin cell from a finger carries the nucleus blueprint for eyes, hair color, fingers — the whole in each part.
- **Ordering force:** like Empedocles' love, Anaxagoras posits an **order** creating animals, humans, flowers, trees — he calls it **mind / intelligence (nous)**.
- **Biographical/doctrinal extras:** first philosopher heard of **in Athens** (came from Asia Minor, moved at age 40); accused of atheism, forced to leave.
- **Sun not a god but a red-hot stone bigger than the Peloponnesian peninsula**; heavenly bodies of the same substance as Earth (from studying a **meteorite**); possible human life on other planets; **Moon has no light of its own — its light comes from Earth** (as the book reports it); explanation of **solar eclipses**; strong interest in astronomy.

## Key terms
Seeds (infinite particles); "something of everything in everything"; nous (mind/intelligence) as ordering force; hologram/cell illustrations.

## Significance / legacy
Infinite-seeds pluralism + explicit cosmic Mind; carries Empedoclean pluralism further while adding intelligence as cosmic orderer.

## Quotes
- "Is there a basic substance that everything else is made of?"
