# 22 - Marx - Alienation History and Capital

*Context: Gaarder presents Marx (1818–1883) as Hegel turned right-side-up — history driven not by World Spirit but by material/economic base, with revolution as cure for alienation and exploitation.*

## 1. Dramatic entry and life coordinates

- Sophie meets Ebenezer Scrooge (Dickens's miserly capitalist) poring over ledgers — "Not a penny must go to waste" — and the little match girl (Andersen) in rags: starving/freezing, first sale in over hundred years, threatens to set fire to woods, reveals herself as communist, "red cock has crowed."
- Moral staged: no reason for match girl to die when Scrooge wealthy. Scrooge: "Justice only exists between equals," "I had to work my way up... Progress." "You don't figure in my accounts! So--be off with you--to the poorhouse!"
- Alberto: appropriate prelude to Marx — tremendous class struggles of mid-19th century.

Biographical points Gaarder gives:

- When Kierkegaard went to Berlin 1841, he might have sat next to Karl Marx at Schelling's lectures.
- Symmetry of theses: Kierkegaard wrote MA thesis on Socrates; Marx wrote doctoral thesis on Democritus and Epicurus — i.e. ancient materialism. Thus both staked out course: Kierkegaard existentialist, Marx materialist (historical materialist).
- Each in own way took Hegel as point of departure, influenced by Hegel's mode of thought, rejected his "world spirit"/idealism as too high-flown.
- Generally: era of great philosophical systems ended with Hegel. After him existential philosophy or philosophy of action. Marx's marker:
> "philosophers have only interpreted the world in various ways; the point is to change it."
- Marx not only philosopher; historian, sociologist, economist. No other philosopher greater significance for practical politics.
- Warning: do not identify everything calling itself Marxism with Marx's own thinking. Said of Marx he only became Marxist mid-1840s, but even after could assert he was not a Marxist. Friend/colleague Friedrich Engels contributed from start. Later Lenin, Stalin, Mao added — Marxism-Leninism.
- Lived last 34 years in London (moved 1849, died 1883). Darwin living just outside London same time (died 1882). Marx wanted to dedicate English edition of *Capital* to Darwin; Darwin declined. Engels at Marx's death: "As Darwin discovered the theory of organic evolution, so Marx discovered the theory of mankind's historical evolution."

## 2. Hegel inversion: historical / dialectical materialism

- Not philosophical materialist like ancient atomists, nor mechanical materialism of 17th–18th centuries.
- Thought to great extent material factors determine way we think. Decisive for historical development.
- Hegel: historical development driven by tension between opposites, resolved by sudden change. Marx developed this further. But Hegel standing on his head.
- Hegel called driver "world spirit" or "world reason." Upside down, says Marx. Material changes affect history. "Spiritual relations" do not create material change; other way about. Material change creates new spiritual relations. Emphasized economic forces.
- Example: antiquity's philosophy/science purely theoretical, nobody interested in putting discoveries into practice. Because economic life organized on slave labor — citizens had no need to increase production with practical innovations. Material relations affect philosophical reflection.

## 3. Base and superstructure — the Greek temple model

Alberto uses miniature Parthenon:

- Roof + front gable = superstructure. Cannot float in thin air. Supported by columns.
- Powerful foundations/bases support entire construction. Likewise material relations support thoughts/ideas. Superstructure is reflection of bases.

Definitions:

> "Marx called these material, economic, and social relations the basis of society. The way a society thinks, what kind of political institutions there are, which laws it has and, not least, what there is of religion, morals, art, philosophy, and science, Marx called society's superstructure."

Three levels in base:

1. **Conditions of production** — natural conditions/resources available. Foundation clearly determines type of production and nature of society/culture. "You can't have a herring trade in the Sahara, or grow dates in northern Norway." Way people think in nomadic culture very different from fishing village in northern Norway.
2. **Means of production** — equipment, tools, machinery, raw materials. Old days rowed to fishing grounds; nowadays huge trawlers.
3. **Owners / production relations** — who owns means, division of labor, distribution of work and ownership.

Consequences:

- Mode of production determines political/ideological conditions. Not by chance we think differently and have different moral codex from old feudal society.
- No eternal natural right. Morally right is product of base. Example: old peasant society parents decided whom children married — question of who inherit farm. Modern city: meet spouse at party/disco, if in love find somewhere to live. Sophie: child of her time.
- Mainly ruling class sets norms for right/wrong.
> "the history of all hitherto existing societies is the history of class struggles." I.e. history principally matter of who owns means of production.
- Thoughts/ideas can help change history? Yes and no. Superstructure can have interactive effect on base, but denied independent history of its own. What drove development from slave society to industrial society primarily determined by changes in base.
- Because interaction/dialectic between base and superstructure acknowledged (Sophie's Plato-vases-wine objection answered: "not that simple," "Plato was neither a potter nor a wine grower"), Marx is dialectical materialist, not mechanical.

## 4. Theory of history: modes and class struggle

- In all phases conflict between two dominant classes:
  - Antiquity slave society: free citizen vs slave.
  - Medieval feudal: feudal lord vs serf; later aristocrat vs citizen.
  - Marx's own bourgeois/capitalist society: capitalists vs workers/proletariat — those who own means vs those who do not.
- Upper classes do not voluntarily relinquish power; change only through revolution.
- Marx especially interested in transition capitalist → communist. But before that, view of labor.

## 5. Labor and alienation

Young Marx preoccupied with what happens to man when he works — something Hegel also analyzed.

- Hegel: interactive/dialectic man–nature. When man alters nature, himself altered. When man works, interacts with nature and transforms it, but nature also interacts and transforms consciousness.
- Marx's point summarized as "Tell me what you do and I'll tell you who you are." How we work affects consciousness, consciousness affects way we work. Hand–consciousness interaction. Way you think closely connected to job you do. Unemployed person in sense empty.
- To Hegel and Marx, work positive, closely connected with essence of mankind.

Capitalist perversion:

> "Under the capitalist system, the worker labors for someone else. His labor is thus something external to him--or something that does not belong to him. The worker becomes alien to his work--but at the same time also alien to himself. He loses touch with his own reality. Marx says, with a Hegelian expression, that the worker becomes alienated."

Illustrations Gaarder uses (not fourfold German-Ideology schema — Gaarder keeps it unitary):

- Aunt packaging candy 20+ years, hates going to work every morning. If she hates work, in sense hates herself. Hates candy for sure.
- Worker slaves for another class, transfers own labor — and whole life — to bourgeoisie.
- Mid-19th-century conditions as point of departure: 12-hour day in freezing production hall, pay so poor children/expectant mothers also had to work, unspeakable conditions, part wages paid in cheap liquor, women obliged to supplement by prostitution, customers respected citizenry. In situation that should be honorable hallmark — work — worker turned into beast of burden.
- Contrast: bourgeois children play violin in warm spacious living rooms after bath, or piano while waiting four-course dinner. Violin/piano could serve as diversion after horseback ride.

### Note on brief's "four alienations" and "opium"

Gaarder in this extraction does **not** subdivide alienation into product / process / species-being / fellow workers, and does **not** use "opium of the people" formula. Religion is treated only as part of superstructure reflecting base. This summary does not import those distinctions beyond what Gaarder states, to stay faithful to the chapter.

## 6. Surplus value / exploitation mechanics

> "Many people still live under inhuman conditions while they continue to produce commodities that make capitalists richer and richer. Marx called this exploitation."

Steps:

1. Worker produces commodity with certain exchange-value.
2. Deduct wages + other production costs → sum left over. This Marx called profit. Capitalist pockets value actually created by worker. That is exploitation.
3. Capitalist invests some profit in new capital — e.g. modernizing plant to produce more cheaply, increase future profit. Seems logical. Long term will not go as imagined.

## 7. Why capitalism is self-destructive

> "Marx believed there were a number of inherent contradictions in the capitalist method of production. Capitalism is an economic system which is self-destructive because it lacks rational control."

Inherent progressive in sense stage on way to communism.

Two vicious circles spelled out:

A. Mechanization → concentration:
- Capitalist has surplus, uses part to modernize, but also spends on violin lessons, wife accustomed to luxury.
- Buys new machinery, no longer needs so many employees, to increase competitive power. Not only one thinking this — production as whole continually more effective. Factories bigger, concentrated in fewer hands. Fewer workers required → more unemployed → increasing social problems/crises signal marching toward destruction.

B. Wage squeeze → underconsumption:
- Profit tied up in means without enough surplus to keep production competitive → facing bankruptcy, cannot buy raw materials.
- Smartest economize: cut wages. If all capitalists as smart — and they are — workers so poor can't afford to buy goods. Purchasing power falling. Vicious circle. "The knell has sounded for capitalist private property." Rapidly approaching revolutionary situation.

Outcome compressed: "in the end the proletariat rises and takes over the means of production."

## 8. Dictatorship of proletariat → classless society

- For period, new "class society" where proletarians suppress bourgeoisie by force — dictatorship of proletariat.
- After transition, replaced by "classless society" where means owned "by all" — people themselves.
- Policy: "from each according to his abilities, to each according to his needs."
- Labor now belongs to workers, capitalism's alienation ceases.

## 9. Gaarder's evaluation and split of socialism

- Yes and no revolution happened. Economists can establish Marx mistaken on vital issues, not least analysis of crises. Paid insufficient attention to plundering natural environment — serious consequences experienced today.
- Nevertheless Marxism led to great upheavals. Socialism largely succeeded combating inhumane society. In Europe live in society with more justice/solidarity than Marx did — not least due to Marx and socialist movement.
- Split after Marx:
  - Social Democracy — gradual peaceful path, Western Europe's way, slow revolution.
  - Leninism — retained belief revolution only way, great influence Eastern Europe, Asia, Africa. Each fought hardship/oppression.
- But created new oppression? e.g. Russia/Eastern Europe — no doubt, everything man touches mixture good/evil. Unreasonable to blame Marx for negative factors 50–100 years after death. Maybe gave too little thought to administrators of communist society. Probably never "promised land." Mankind always creates new problems.

*Manifesto* quotes Gaarder gives:

> "A spectre is haunting Europe--the spectre of Communism."

> "The Communists disdain to conceal their views and aims. They openly declare that their ends can be attained only by the forcible overthrow of all existing social conditions. Let the ruling classes tremble at a Communist revolution. The proletarians have nothing to lose but their chains. They have a world to win. Workingmen of all countries, unite!"

## 10. Justice coda — Rawls (in Marx chapter)

Scrooge said "Justice only exists between equals" — Alberto turns into question: how define just society? Via moral philosopher John Rawls:

- Imagine member of distinguished council to make all laws for future society. Must consider every detail, because as soon as agreement signed, all drop dead — then immediately come to life again in society legislated for, with no idea which position will have.
- That would be just society, arisen among equals. Men and women — none knew whether wake as man or woman, odds fifty-fifty, society just as attractive for women as men.
- Was Europe of Marx such society? Absolutely not. Do we know such today? "Think about it."
