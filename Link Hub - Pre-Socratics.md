# Link Hub - Pre-Socratics

Table of all 8 natural philosophers with one-line theses:

| Philosopher | One-line thesis |
|---|---|
| [[Thales]] | The source of all things is water. |
| [[Anaximander]] | Our world is one of myriad worlds in the boundless (apeiron). |
| [[Anaximenes]] | The source of all things is air/vapor via condensation and rarefaction. |
| [[Parmenides]] | Nothing changes; being has always existed; trust reason over senses. |
| [[Heraclitus]] | Everything flows; opposites constitute the world under universal logos. |
| [[Empedocles]] | Four everlasting roots (earth, air, fire, water) joined/separated by love and strife. |
| [[Anaxagoras]] | Infinite seeds ("something of everything in everything") ordered by Mind (nous). |
| [[Democritus]] | Everything is eternal, indivisible atoms + void moving by necessity. |

## Lineage
Milesians → Eleatics → pluralists → atomists:
- **Milesians** ([[Thales]] → [[Anaximander]] → [[Anaximenes]]): one single basic substance; each revises the arché (water → the boundless → air) and Anaximenes adds a mechanism (condensation/rarefaction).
- **Eleatics** ([[Parmenides]] vs [[Heraclitus]]): the one-substance view breaks on the problem of change — Parmenides denies change (reason over senses) while Heraclitus affirms universal flux (senses reliable, logos abides).
- **Pluralists** ([[Empedocles]] → [[Anaxagoras]]): reconcile both by multiplying the permanent — four roots + love/strife, then infinite seeds + nous.
- **Atomists** ([[Democritus]]): final answer — eternal indivisible atoms + void; permanence at base, flux of combinations; materialism and necessity.

## Key debate
- **One vs many:** Milesians posit one arché; pluralists/atomists answer that one element cannot become fish, butterfly, or watermelon — many permanents are needed.
- **Reason vs senses:** [[Parmenides]] trusts reason against the eyes (change is illusion); [[Heraclitus]] trusts the eyes (flow is real, logos its law); [[Empedocles]] and [[Democritus]] split the difference (senses true of compounds, reason true of basics).
- **Change vs permanence:** nothing comes from nothing and nothing becomes nothing — so what endures (water? the boundless? air? Being? roots? seeds? atoms?) and what merely combines, separates, and re-forms (rivers, flowers, cakes, bodies, worlds)?
