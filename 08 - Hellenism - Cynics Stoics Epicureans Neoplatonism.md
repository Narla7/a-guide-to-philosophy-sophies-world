# 08 - Hellenism: Cynics, Stoics, Epicureans, Neoplatonism

*Context: From Aristotle's death (322 B.C.) to ~A.D. 400 — Greek philosophy becomes practical ethics and salvation in a cosmopolitan, religiously fused world.*

## 1. Historical Setting: Alexander to Rome

- **End of the city-state:** Aristotle dies 322 B.C. as Athens loses dominance, largely through upheavals from **Alexander the Great (356–323 B.C.)**, King of Macedonia. Aristotle himself, a Macedonian, was for a time the young Alexander's tutor.
- **Alexander's conquests:** Final decisive victory over Persians; links Egypt and the Orient as far east as India to Greek civilization.
- **Hellenism (~300 years):** Greek culture and language lead in three Hellenistic kingdoms — **Macedonia, Syria, Egypt**. Term means both period and Greek-dominated culture.
- **Rome takes over (~50 B.C. onward):** Rome secures military/political upper hand, conquers all Hellenistic kingdoms. Roman culture/Latin predominate from Spain to Asia — the **Roman period / Late Antiquity**. Crucial qualification: before conquering the Hellenistic world, Rome itself was a province of Greek culture. Greek philosophy therefore outlives Greek political power.

## 2. Religion, Philosophy, Science Fused

- **Borders erased:** Greeks, Romans, Egyptians, Babylonians, Syrians, Persians previously had "national religions." Now cultures merge into "one great witch's caldron" of religious, philosophical and scientific ideas.
- **Town square → world arena:** Old square buzzed with wares and ideas; new squares fill with wares and ideas from all over the world, in many languages.
- **Syncretism:** Greek view of life spreads wider than before, but Oriental gods are worshipped all over the Mediterranean. New formations draw on gods/beliefs of many old nations = **syncretism / fusion of creeds**.
- **Mood:** Loss of folk/city-state affinity → doubt, uncertainty, cultural dissolution, pessimism. Saying: **"the world has grown old."**
- **Salvation religions:** Common feature — teachings on how mankind attains salvation from death, often secret. By teachings + rituals, hope for immortality of soul and eternal life. Insight into true nature of universe can matter as much as ritual.
- **Philosophy as salvation:** Philosophy moves toward "salvation" and serenity. Insight not only its own reward; must free from pessimism and fear of death. Boundaries between religion and philosophy gradually eliminated.
- **Unoriginal but ethical:** No new Plato or Aristotle. Three great Athenians inspire trends. Common project inherited from Socrates/Plato/Aristotle: **how mankind should best live and die — ethics. What is true happiness and how achieved?** Four trends examined.
- **Science:** Blend from various cultures. **Athens remains philosophy center** with functioning schools after Plato/Aristotle. **Alexandria becomes science center** — meeting East-West, extensive library, mathematics, astronomy, biology, medicine.
- **Parallel to today:** Gaarder explicitly compares to 20th century open civilization, upheavals in religion/philosophy, Greek/Egyptian/Oriental religions in Rome then as world religions in European cities now; old+new conglomerate on "view-of-life" market; much "new knowledge" is flotsam of old thought rooted in Hellenism.

## 3. The Cynics

- **Motto:** Socrates gazing at market stall: **"What a lot of things I don't need!"**
- **Founder:** **Antisthenes in Athens ~400 B.C.**, pupil of Socrates, interested especially in his frugality.
- **Core doctrine:** True happiness not in external advantages — material luxury, political power, good health. Happiness is not being dependent on such random, fleeting things. Therefore within everyone's reach, and once attained can never be lost.
- **Diogenes (pupil of Antisthenes):** Best known. Reputedly lived in a barrel, owned only cloak, stick, bread bag — so happiness hard to steal. Visited by Alexander the Great enjoying sun: asked if anything desired — **"Yes. Stand to one side. You're blocking the sun."** Shows himself no less happy/rich than emperor; had everything desired.
- **Attitude to suffering:** Need not be concerned about own health; even suffering and death should not disturb; nor torment themselves over others' woes.
- **Modern shift:** "Cynical/cynicism" now means sneering disbelief in sincerity and insensitivity to suffering — not original meaning.

## 4. The Stoics

- **Origin:** Cynics instrumental. Founded Athens **~300 B.C. by Zeno**, from Cyprus, joined Cynics after shipwreck. Gathered followers under a **portico (stoa)** → "Stoic." Later great significance for Roman culture.
- **Logos / microcosm-macrocosm:** Like Heraclitus, everyone part of same common sense — **logos**. Each person a world in miniature (**microcosmos**) reflecting the **macro-cosmos**.
- **Natural law:** Hence universal right-ness — **natural law** based on timeless human/universal reason, does not alter with time/place. With Socrates against Sophists. Governs all mankind, even slaves. Legal statutes of states merely incomplete imitations of law embedded in nature.
- **Monism:** Erase individual/universe divide; deny spirit vs matter conflict. Only one nature. **Monism** vs Plato's clear dualism/two-fold reality.
- **Cosmopolitanism:** True children of time — "cosmopolitan," more receptive than "barrel philosophers." Emphasize human fellowship, preoccupied with politics; many active statesmen, notably **Emperor Marcus Aurelius (A.D. 121–180)**. Encouraged Greek culture/philosophy in Rome. **Cicero (106–43 B.C.)** — orator/philosopher/statesman — forms concept of **humanism**: view with individual as central focus. Later **Seneca (4 B.C.–A.D. 65)**: **"to mankind, mankind is holy"** — slogan of humanism ever since.
- **Fate and acceptance:** All natural processes — sickness, death — follow unbreakable laws of nature. Learn to accept destiny. Nothing accidental, everything through necessity; little use complaining when fate knocks. Accept happy events unperturbed too. Kinship with Cynics: external events unimportant. Survival: **"stoic calm"** for one not letting feelings take over.

## 5. The Epicureans

- **Socratic root via Aristippus:** Socrates sought good life. Aristippus (another pupil) held aim is highest possible sensory enjoyment: **"The highest good is pleasure, the greatest evil is pain."** Develop life to avoid pain in all forms. Cynics/Stoics believed in enduring pain — not same as setting out to avoid it.
- **Epicurus (341–270):** Founds school Athens ~300 B.C. Followers Epicureans. Combines Aristippus pleasure ethic with **Democritus atom theory**.
- **The Garden:** Lived in a garden — "garden philosophers." Entrance notice: **"Stranger, here you will live well. Here pleasure is the highest good."**
- **Pleasure calculus:**
  - Weigh pleasurable results against side effects. Example: binge 200 crowns' chocolate at once — half hour later understand side effects.
  - Weigh short-term pleasure against greater/lasting/intense long-term pleasure. Abstain chocolate year to save for bike or trip to England. Unlike animals, we plan lives — make "pleasure calculation."
- **Pleasure redefined:** Not necessarily sensual (chocolate). Friendship, appreciation of art count. Enjoyment requires old Greek ideals: **self-control, temperance, serenity**. Curb desire; serenity helps endure pain.
- **Gods and death:** Fear of gods brought many to garden. Democritus atoms cure superstition. Overcome fear of death via "soul atoms" dispersing at death — no life after death. Quote: **"Death does not concern us, because as long as we exist, death is not here. And when it does come, we no longer exist."** No one ever bothered by being dead.
- **Four medicinal herbs (liberating summary / philosophic medicine chest, medicine analogy Greek):**
  1. The gods are not to be feared.
  2. Death is nothing to worry about.
  3. Good is easy to attain.
  4. The fearful is easy to endure.
- **Politics:** Vs Stoics, little/no interest in politics/community. **"Live in seclusion!"** Garden likened to present-day communes / "safe harbor" away from society.
- **Degeneration:** After Epicurus, overemphasis on self-indulgence — "Live for the moment!" Modern "epicurean" negatively = one who lives only for pleasure.

## 6. Neoplatonism

- **Place:** Cynicism/Stoicism/Epicureanism rooted in Socrates (+ Heraclitus, Democritus). Most remarkable late trend inspired first and foremost by Plato → **Neoplatonism**.
- **Plotinus (c. 205–270):** Most important figure. Studied Alexandria, later settled Rome. Significant: from Alexandria, meeting point for Greek philosophy and Oriental mysticism for centuries. Brings doctrine of salvation competing seriously with Christianity; also becomes strong influence in mainstream Christian theology.
- **Platonic base:** Plato's ideas vs sensory world; clear soul/body division. Man dual: body earth/dust, plus immortal soul — widely believed Greeks long before Plato; Plotinus also knows similar Asian ideas.
- **The One and emanation:** World spans two poles. One end divine light called **the One**, sometimes God. Other end absolute darkness receiving none of the light. Darkness has no existence — simply absence of light — i.e. is not. All that exists is God/the One, but as beam grows dimmer and extinguished, somewhere divine glow cannot reach.
- **Soul/matter/forms:** Soul illuminated by light from One; matter is darkness with no real existence. Forms in nature have faint glow of One.
- **Bonfire metaphor (central):** Reality = great burning bonfire at night, sparks fly. Wide radius turns night to day nearby; glow visible miles away; further tiny speck like far-off lantern; further still light does not reach; rays disappear; complete dark sees nothing, neither shapes nor shadows. Burning = God; darkness beyond = cold matter man/animals made of. Closest to God: eternal ideas, primal forms of all creatures. Human soul above all a **"spark from the fire."** Everywhere in nature some divine light shines — rose, bluebell, sunflower, poppy, butterfly from twig, goldfish in bowl. Furthest: earth, water, stone. Closest to God in own soul; only there become one with mystery; rare moments experience we ourselves are divine mystery.
- **Vs Plato's cave:** Like myth of cave — closer to mouth, closer to source of existence. But vs Plato's clear two-fold reality, Plotinus wholeness: **everything is one — for everything is God.** Even shadows deep in cave have faint glow of One.
- **Mystical fusion:** On rare occasions Plotinus experienced fusion of soul with God.

## 7. Mysticism (General Doctrine)

- **Definition:** Experience of merging with God or "cosmic spirit." Many religions emphasize gulf between God and Creation; mystic experiences no gulf — "one with God," "merging."
- **The two I's:** What we call "I" is not true "I." Short glimpses — identification with greater "I" — God, cosmic spirit, Nature, Universe. Fusion = "losing oneself," disappearing/lost in God as drop loses itself merging with sea. Indian mystic: **"When I was, God was not. When God is, I am no more."** Christian **Angelus Silesius (1624–1677)**: *Every drop becomes the sea when it flows oceanward, just as at last the soul ascends and thus becomes the Lord.*
- **Loss vs gain:** Lose only momentary form, gain much bigger: you are universe, cosmic spirit itself, God. Everyday I lost anyway; real "I" mystics describe as mysterious fire burning eternally.
- **Path:** Does not always come itself. Seek path of **"purification and enlightenment"** — simple life + meditation techniques. Then suddenly: **"I am God"** or **"I am You."**
- **Universality + cultural interpretation:** Mystical trends in all great world religions; descriptions remarkably similar across boundaries. Cultural background reveals itself in religious/philosophic interpretation of experience.
- **West vs East:** Western (Judaism, Christianity, Islam) — meeting with **personal God**; present in nature/soul yet far above/beyond world. Eastern (Hinduism, Buddhism, Chinese) — total fusion with God/"cosmic spirit": **"I am the cosmic spirit," "I am God."** God not only present in world; nowhere else to be.
- **Indian roots:** Strong movements since long before Plato. **Swami Vivekananda**, brought Hinduism west: *"Just as certain world religions say that people who do not believe in a personal God outside themselves are atheists, we say that a person who does not believe in himself is an atheist. Not believing in the splendor of one's own soul is what we call atheism."*
- **Ethics:** Former Indian president **Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan**: *"Love thy neighbor as thyself because you are your neighbor. It is an illusion that makes you think that your neighbor is someone other than yourself."*
- **Modern secular:** Non-religious today report "cosmic consciousness" or "oceanic feeling" — wrenched out of Time, experiencing world "from perspective of eternity."
