# Novalis

*Context: German Romanticism (late 18th–mid 19th c.), Universal Romanticism around Jena c. 1800. Reaction to Enlightenment reason and [[18 - Kant - Phenomena Duty and Categorical Imperative|Kant]]'s cool intellectualism. Catchwords: **feeling, imagination, experience, yearning**.*

## 1. World-dream

- Young genius: *the world becomes a dream, and the dream becomes reality.*
- Artist had **universe-creating imagination**. In transports of artistic rapture could sense dissolving boundary between dream and reality.
- Only art could bring us closer to the inexpressible. Some went as far compare artist to God, because artist creates own reality way God created world.
- English Romantic **Coleridge** same idea:
> What if you slept? And what if, in your sleep, you dreamed? And what if, in your dream, you went to heaven and there plucked a strange and beautiful flower? And what if, when you awoke, you had the flower in your hand? Ah, what then?

## 2. Blue flower

- Novel *Heinrich von Ofterdingen* set Medieval times, unfinished when died 1801, very significant.
- Young Heinrich searching for **blue flower** once saw in dream and yearned ever since — symbol distant unattainable.
- Yearning for something distant unattainable characteristic of Romanticism.
- Byron's true love likewise illusive unattainable as Novalis's blue flower.

## 3. Inwards / world-soul link

- ***The path of mystery leads inwards.*** Man bears whole universe within himself and comes closest to mystery stepping inside himself.
- [[Schelling]] link: world spirit can be sought both in nature and own mind.
- Nature-philosophy context: nature is nothing but one big I; world soul / world spirit.

## 4. Sophie

- Engaged to fourteen-year-old girl Sophie, died four days after fifteenth birthday, remained devoted rest short life.
- Novalis died only twenty-nine. One of young dead. Many died young usually tuberculosis, some suicide. Those lived old usually stopped being Romantics about thirty, some became thoroughly middle-class conservative — went over to enemy.

## 5. Wider Romantic traits he exemplifies

- Longed for bygone eras such as Middle Ages, enthusiastically reappraised after Enlightenment negative evaluation. Longed distant cultures like Orient with mysticism. Drawn to Night, Twilight, old ruins and supernatural. Preoccupied with dark side of life, murky, uncanny, mystical.
- Sociology: urban, young men/students c. 1800, anti-middle class (philistines), first student uprising, hippie parallel; *idleness is ideal of genius, and indolence virtue of Romantic.*

## Quotes

- The world becomes a dream, and the dream becomes reality.
- The path of mystery leads inwards.
