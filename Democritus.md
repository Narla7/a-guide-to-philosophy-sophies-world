# Democritus
*Pre-Socratic natural philosopher — from Sophie's World, extreme-detail summary. Chapter context: first systematic philosophers ask — is there a basic substance everything is made of, and how can it change? Final natural-philosopher answer: atom theory. If you grasp the **Lego** question, you grasp him.*

The natural philosophers were the earliest Greek philosophers, each with his own "project" — what he was especially concerned to find out. They assumed "something" had always existed and asked what basic substance lies at the root of all change, seeking underlying laws by studying nature itself rather than myths. Only fragments survive, mostly via Aristotle two centuries later, who reports conclusions more than paths; what matters is the questions they asked and the type of answer they sought.

## Core thesis
Everything is built of tiny invisible blocks, each **eternal and immutable** = **atoms**. **"A-tom" = "un-cuttable."** Democritus of Abdera, northern Aegean (c. 460–370 B.C.).

## Arguments / reasoning (full detail, all examples from book)
- Atoms cannot be divided indefinitely, else they couldn't serve as building blocks and nature would dissolve "like constantly diluted soup."
- The blocks must be eternal because **nothing can come from nothing** (with Parmenides and the Eleatics); firm and solid; but **not all identical** — else no explaining poppies, olive trees, goatskin, human hair. Hence an **unlimited number and variety**: some round and smooth, others irregular and jagged — differences allow joining into all kinds of bodies, yet all eternal, immutable, indivisible.
- Bodies (tree, animal) die and disintegrate → atoms disperse and are reused in new bodies. Atoms move in space; with **"hooks" and "barbs"** they join into all visible things.
- **Lego analogy (the book's own, allowed as philosophical illustration):** Lego has the same properties — **indivisible**, different shapes and sizes, solid and impermeable, hooks and barbs to connect into every conceivable figure, breakable apart for new figures, reusable — "eternal" (today's children play with their parents' blocks). Contrast **clay**: breakable into ever-smaller pieces, never rejoinable into something else.
- **Modern vindication note (the book's):** nature really is built of different atoms joining and separating — a hydrogen atom at the tip of your nose was once in an elephant's trunk; a carbon atom in your cardiac muscle was once in a dinosaur's tail. Today atoms are divisible into **protons, neutrons, electrons**, perhaps further, but physicists agree there must be a **limit / minimal part**.
- **Method:** no electronic apparatus — mind alone; reason left no choice once accepting that nothing changes (at base), nothing comes from nothing, nothing is lost → infinitesimal rejoinable blocks.
- **Materialism + mechanism:** no "force" or "soul" intervening; only **atoms and the void** → **materialist**. No conscious design in atomic motion; all happens **mechanically** — not randomly but by **inevitable laws of necessity**; every happening has a natural cause inherent in the thing. "Would rather discover a new cause of nature than be King of Persia."
- **Sense perception:** sensing = movement of atoms in space; seeing the moon = **"moon atoms" penetrating the eye**.
- **Soul:** the soul too can consist of atoms — special round, smooth **"soul atoms"**; at death they fly in all directions and may enter a new soul formation. Hence **no immortal soul** — "soul" tied to the brain, no consciousness once the brain disintegrates (a view many share today, per the book).
- **Synthesis formula:** agrees with Heraclitus that everything "flows" (forms come and go) but behind the flow are eternal non-flowing things = atoms. Marks the end of Greek natural philosophy "for the time being."
- **Sophie's own rehearsal (in-book reasoning, kept as philosophy):** plain water never turns into more than ice/steam — not even a watermelon (watermelons contain more than water); Parmenides courageous to deny visible change; Empedocles smart to require more than one substance; philosophy not learnable as facts but as learning to **think philosophically** with common sense.

## Key terms
Atoms / void / materialism / necessity; "un-cuttable"; hooks and barbs; Lego/clay illustrations; soul atoms; moon atoms; like-perceives-like background.

## Significance / legacy
Atom theory as the culmination of natural philosophy: saves permanence (atoms) and change (combinations), agrees with Heraclitus on flux of forms and with Parmenides on eternity at base; precursor of science; forward links to Epicureans, Aristotle's criticism, and Darwin's material lineage.
