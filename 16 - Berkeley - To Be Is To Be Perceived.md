# 16 - Berkeley - To Be Is To Be Perceived

*Context: Sophie's empiricist sequence reaches George Berkeley, who turns empiricism against matter itself.*

## 1. Life and motive

- **George Berkeley (1685–1753)**, Irish bishop — Bishop of Cloyne.
- Also philosopher; main work given in Hilde's encyclopedia as **A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge (1710)**.
- Motive: felt current philosophies and science were threat to Christian way of life; all-pervading **materialism** threatened Christian faith in God as creator and preserver of all nature.
- Paradox: yet most consistent of the empiricists.

## 2. Core: immaterialism — esse est percipi

- Holds empiricist premise: cannot know more of world than perceive through senses — then goes further.
- Claim: worldly things **are indeed as we perceive them, but they are not "things."**
- Only things that exist are those we perceive. We do **not** perceive "material" or "matter." We do not perceive things as tangible objects.
- To assume what we perceive has underlying "substance" is jumping to conclusions; absolutely no experience to base it on.

## 3. Against Locke: primary qualities and material substance

- Recalled Locke division:
  - Secondary qualities (green, sour of apple): cannot state things *are* so, only we perceive so.
  - Primary qualities (density, gravity, weight): Locke said really belong to external reality, which has material substance. Locke, like Descartes and Spinoza, believed material world is reality.
- Berkeley questions exactly this, by logic of empiricism:
  - No impression of matter itself, only of qualities perceived.
  - Therefore material substance is unwarranted inference.

## 4. Table-thump, dreams, hypnosis: objections and replies

- Common-sense objection acted out: Sophie thumps fist on table — "Ouch, doesn't that prove table really table, both material and matter?"
- Reply: How felt it? Felt **something hard** — had sensation of hard, didn't feel actual matter in table.
  - Can dream hitting something hard with nothing hard in dream.
  - Hypnotized person can be made to "feel" warmth/cold, caress/punch without those objects.
- Question "if table wasn't really hard, why did I feel it?" leads to spiritual cause theory.

## 5. Spirit as cause: God perceiver guaranteeing continuity

- Berkeley believes in **"spirit"**: all our ideas have cause beyond consciousness, but cause not material — spiritual.
- My own soul can cause my own ideas — as when dreaming.
- Only **another will or spirit** can cause ideas making up "corporeal" world. Everything due to that spirit which is cause of "everything in everything" and which "all things consist in."
- That spirit = **God**:
  - "we can moreover claim that the existence of God is far more clearly perceived than the existence of man."
  - Everything seen/felt is "an effect of God's power."
  - God is "intimately present in our consciousness, causing to exist for us the profusion of ideas and perceptions that we are constantly subject to."
  - Whole world and whole life exist in God. He is one cause of everything. We exist only in mind of God.
- Hence continuity problem solved theistically: things persist because perceived by God even when not perceived by us.

## 6. Time, space, self: who are we?

- Material reality not only thing questioned: **time and space** have no absolute/independent existence either. Our perception of time/space can be figments of mind. A week or two for us need not be week or two for God.
- Reframed Hamlet: "'to be or not to be' is not whole question. Question is also who we are. Are we really human beings of flesh and blood? Does our world consist of real things — or are we encircled by the mind?"
- Formula implied in dialogue: **to be is to be perceived**. Later reprise: "For we cannot perceive the matter itself that our reality is made of ... According to Berkeley, all we can know is that we are spirit."
- Hilde's encyclopedia gloss (quoted in novel): "Denied existence of a material world beyond the human mind. Our sense perceptions proceed from God."
