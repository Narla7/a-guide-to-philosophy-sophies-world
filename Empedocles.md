# Empedocles
*Pre-Socratic natural philosopher — from Sophie's World, extreme-detail summary. Chapter context: first systematic philosophers ask — is there a basic substance everything is made of, and how can it change? Empedocles of Sicily reconciles Parmenides (reason/permanence) with Heraclitus (senses/flux) by pluralism.*

The natural philosophers were the earliest Greek philosophers, each with his own "project" — what he was especially concerned to find out. They assumed "something" had always existed and asked what basic substance lies at the root of all change, seeking underlying laws by studying nature itself rather than myths. Only fragments survive, mostly via Aristotle two centuries later, who reports conclusions more than paths; what matters is the questions they asked and the type of answer they sought.

## Core thesis
Nature = **four everlasting "roots"**: **earth, air, fire, water**; all processes = their **coming together and separating** in varying proportions. Plus two forces: **love** (binds) and **strife** (separates). He distinguishes **substance vs force**. Empedocles of Sicily (c. 490–430 B.C.) — the reconciler.

## Arguments / reasoning (full detail, all examples from book)
- **Reconciliation argument:** Parmenides says (a) nothing can change, (b) the senses are unreliable. Heraclitus says (a) everything changes, (b) the senses are reliable. They disagree maximally because both assume **only one element**.
- Empedocles: each is right once and wrong once. Water alone cannot turn into a fish or a butterfly; pure water stays pure water — so Parmenides is right that "nothing [basic] changes." But the senses truly show nature changing — so Heraclitus is right to trust the senses.
- Solution: reject the single substance. The four roots are everlastingly "untouched"; compounds (flower, animal) form and separate; on death the four separate again, registrable by the naked eye. So "not everything changes": the basics don't; combinations do.
- **Painting:** with only red you cannot paint green trees; with yellow, red, blue, and black you can mix hundreds of colors in varying proportions.
- **Kitchen:** with only flour, baking a cake takes a wizard; with eggs, flour, milk, and sugar, any number of cakes.
- **Burning wood:** the crackle/splutter = "water" being released; the smoke rising = "air"; the visible flame = "fire"; the ash remainder = "earth."
- Why these four? Thales and Anaximenes showed water and air essential; the Greeks saw fire as essential (sun for life, body heat in animals and humans).
- **Forces:** what combines and dissolves e.g. a flower? **Love binds, strife separates.** The substance/force distinction is explicitly linked forward to modern science (elements + natural forces).
- **Theory of perception:** eyes consist of earth, air, fire, and water like everything else; **like perceives like** — the earth in the eye perceives earth outside, air perceives air, etc. Lacking one substance, we would not see all of nature.
- Sophie's rehearsal (kept as philosophy): Empedocles was smart to require more than one substance; philosophy is not learnable as facts but as learning to **think philosophically** with common sense.

## Key terms
Four roots (earth, air, fire, water); love and strife; substance vs force; like-perceives-like; painter/kitchen/burning-wood illustrations.

## Significance / legacy
Pluralism saves both reason and observation; introduces the elements + forces schema; paves the way for Anaxagoras and Democritus.

## Quotes
- "...nothing can come from nothing..." (the Parmenidean constraint he preserves).
- "Can water turn into wine? How can earth and water produce a live frog!" (the guiding puzzle he answers).
