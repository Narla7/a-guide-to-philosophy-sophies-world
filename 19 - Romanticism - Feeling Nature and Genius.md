# 19 - Romanticism - Feeling, Nature and Genius

Context: Gaarder presents Romanticism (late 18th-mid 19th c.) as reaction to Enlightenment/Kant, exalting feeling, genius, organism and world-soul.

## 1. Place in History

- **Europe's last great cultural epoch / last common approach to life.** Began toward end 18th c., lasted till middle 19th. Started in Germany.
- After 1850 can no longer speak of whole epochs comprising poetry, philosophy, art, science, music.
- Previously covered: Renaissance, Baroque, Enlightenment. Now approaching end long story.
- Reaction to Enlightenment's unequivocal emphasis on reason. After Kant and his cool intellectualism, as if German youth heaved sigh of relief.
- New catchwords: **feeling, imagination, experience, yearning**. Enlightenment thinkers like Rousseau had drawn attention to feeling as criticism of bias toward reason; what had been undercurrent now became mainstream German culture.

### Ambivalent heir of Kant
- Many Romantics saw themselves as Kant's successors: Kant established limit what can know of das Ding an sich.
- Also underlined ego's contribution to cognition. Individual now completely free to interpret life own way.
- Romantics exploited in almost unrestrained **ego-worship**, leading to exaltation of artistic genius.

## 2. Genius, Art and the Inexpressible

- Example: **Beethoven**. Music expresses own feelings and yearnings. In sense free artist - unlike Baroque masters Bach and Handel who composed to glory of God mostly in strict musical forms. Moonlight Sonata romantic, Fifth Symphony dramatic self-expression.
- Similarity Renaissance humanists individualists. Typical: importance of art to human cognition.
- Kant contribution: aesthetics - what happens when overwhelmed by beauty - work of art. When abandon ourselves with no other intention than aesthetic experience itself, brought closer to experience of das Ding an sich.
- According to Kant artist plays freely on faculty of cognition. Poet **Schiller** develops: activity of artist like playing, **man only free when he plays, because then he makes up own rules.**
- Romantics: only art could bring us closer to the inexpressible. Some went as far compare artist to God, because artist creates own reality way God created world.
- Said artist had **universe-creating imagination**. In transports of artistic rapture could sense dissolving boundary between dream and reality.
- **Novalis** (young genius): *the world becomes a dream, and the dream becomes reality.* Novel *Heinrich von Ofterdingen* set Medieval times, unfinished when died 1801, very significant. Young Heinrich searching for **blue flower** once saw in dream and yearned ever since - symbol distant unattainable.
- English Romantic **Coleridge** same idea:
> What if you slept? And what if, in your sleep, you dreamed? And what if, in your dream, you went to heaven and there plucked a strange and beautiful flower? And what if, when you awoke, you had the flower in your hand? Ah, what then?

## 3. Longing, Night and Dark Side

- Yearning for something distant unattainable characteristic.
- Longed for bygone eras such as Middle Ages, enthusiastically reappraised after Enlightenment negative evaluation. Longed distant cultures like Orient with mysticism. Drawn to Night, Twilight, old ruins and supernatural. Preoccupied with dark side of life, murky, uncanny, mystical.
- Landscape paintings: dark forests, wild rugged nature, swirling mists - visual counterpart.
- **Romantic love / unrequited love:**
  - Introduced 1774 by **Goethe** in *The Sorrows of Young Werther*. Ends young Werther shooting himself when can't have woman loves. Suicide rate rose after publication, banned time Denmark/Norway. Not without danger. Strong emotions.
  - **Byron and Shelley** Romantic poets so-called Satanic school. Byron provided idol **Byronic hero** - alien, moody, rebellious spirit - in life as art. Willful passionate handsome besieged by women fashion, gossip attributed verses adventures to own life, numerous liaisons but true love illusive unattainable as Novalis's blue flower.
  - Novalis engaged to fourteen-year-old girl Sophie, died four days after fifteenth birthday, remained devoted rest short life. Novalis died only twenty-nine. One of young dead. Many died young usually tuberculosis, some suicide. Those lived old usually stopped being Romantics about thirty, some became thoroughly middle-class conservative - went over to enemy.

## 4. Sociology of Romanticism

- Mainly urban phenomenon. First half last century flourishing metropolitan culture many parts Europe, not least Germany. Typical Romantics young men, often university students, not always serious studies. Decidedly anti-middle class: refer police or landladies as philistines or enemy.
- First generation young about 1800, actually Europe's first student uprising. Not unlike hippies 150 years later: flower power long hair strumming guitars lying around.
- Said: *idleness is ideal of genius, and indolence virtue of Romantic.* Duty to experience life - or dream away from it. Day-to-day business taken care of by philistines.

## 5. Nature-Philosophy: Organism vs Mechanism

- Represents not least reaction to Enlightenment mechanistic universe. Said Romanticism implied renaissance of old cosmic consciousness.
- Means **viewing nature as whole**. Tracing roots not only back Spinoza, but Plotinus and Renaissance philosophers Jakob Böhme and Giordano Bruno. Common: experienced divine ego in nature. Pantheists.
- Descartes and Hume had drawn sharp line between ego and extended reality. Kant left sharp distinction between cognitive I and nature in itself. Now said **nature is nothing but one big I**. Also used expressions **world soul / world spirit**.
- Leading philosopher **Schelling (1775-1854)**: wanted unite mind and matter. All nature - human soul and physical reality - expression of one Absolute, or world spirit.
  - *Nature is visible spirit, spirit is invisible nature*, since senses structuring spirit everywhere.
  - *Matter is slumbering intelligence.*
  - Saw same world spirit in nature and human mind. Natural and spiritual actually expressions same thing.
  - World spirit can thus be sought both in nature and own mind. Novalis: ***the path of mystery leads inwards.*** Man bears whole universe within himself and comes closest to mystery stepping inside himself.
- For many, philosophy, nature study, poetry formed synthesis. Sitting attic dashing inspired verses and investigating life plants or composition rocks only two sides same coin because nature not dead mechanism, one living world spirit.
- Norwegian-born naturalist **Henrik Steffens** - Wergeland called Norway's departed laurel leaf because settled Germany - went Copenhagen 1801 lecture German Romanticism. Characterized: *Tired of eternal efforts to fight our way through raw matter, we chose another way and sought to embrace the infinite. We went inside ourselves and created a new world...*
- Schelling saw development in nature from earth and rock to human mind. Very gradual transitions inanimate to complicated life forms.
- Characteristic Romantic view: **nature as organism**, unity constantly developing innate potentialities. Like flower unfolding leaves/petals. Or poet unfolding verses.
- Reminds Aristotle: Aristotelian as well as Neoplatonic overtones. Aristotle more organic view than mechanical materialists.

## 6. History, Herder and National Spirit

- Similar ideas in history. **Johann Gottfried von Herder (1744-1803)** great significance. Believed history characterized continuity, evolution, design. **Dynamic** view as process.
- Enlightenment often static view: only one universal reason which could be more/less at periods. Herder: each historical epoch own intrinsic value and each nation own character or soul. Question whether can identify with other cultures.
- Like must identify with another person's situation to understand them, must identify with other cultures. Taken granted nowadays, new then.
- Helped strengthen feeling national identity. No coincidence Norwegian struggle national independence flourished particular time - 1814.

### Universal vs National Romanticism
- Because orientations many areas, distinguish two forms, united by key word **organism**:

1. **Universal Romanticism:** preoccupied nature, world soul, artistic genius. Flourished first, especially around 1800 in Jena.
2. **National Romanticism:** popular little later, especially Heidelberg. Mainly interested history of people, language of people, culture of people. People seen as organism unfolding innate potentiality - exactly like nature and history.

- No sharp dividing line. World spirit just as much present in people/popular culture as nature/art. Considered plant, nation, poetic work, language, entire physical world one organism.

### Folk culture collecting
- Herder forerunner, collecting folk songs many lands *Voices of the People*. Referred folktales as mother tongue of people.
- Brothers Grimm and others began collect folk songs/fairy tales Heidelberg - Grimm's Fairy Tales: Snow White Seven Dwarfs, Rumpelstiltskin, Frog Prince, Hansel Gretel, many more.
- Norway Asbjørnsen and Moe traveled around collecting folks' own tales. Like harvesting juicy fruit suddenly discovered good/nourishing. Urgent - fruit already begun fall. Folk songs collected; Norwegian language began studied scientifically. Old myths/sagas heathen times rediscovered, composers all over Europe began incorporate folk melodies to bridge folk music and art music.
- **Art music** = composed particular person like Beethoven. **Folk music** = not written particular person, came from people, unknown date. Same folktales vs art tales. Art tales written author like Hans Christian Andersen.
- Fairy tale genre passionately cultivated. German master **E.T.A. Hoffmann** - Tales of Hoffmann.
- Fairy tale absolute literary ideal Romantics - same way absolute art form Baroque theater. Gave poet full scope explore own creativity. Could play God to fictional universe.

## 7. Fichte, Schelling's Dark God and Romantic Irony

- Sum-up philosophers: viewed world soul as ego which in more/less dreamlike state created everything. **Fichte** said nature stems from higher, unconscious imagination. **Schelling** said explicitly world is in God. God aware some of it, but other aspects represent unknown in God. For **God also has dark side**. Fascinating frightening, reminds Berkeley.
- Relationship artist/work same light. Fairy tale gave writer free rein to exploit universe-creating imagination. Creative act not always completely conscious. Writer could experience story written by innate force. Practically hypnotic trance while wrote.
- Then suddenly destroy illusion. Intervene story address ironic comments to reader, so reader momentarily reminded after all only story.
- At same time remind reader he manipulating fictional universe. This disillusion called **romantic irony**. Example Henrik Ibsen lets character in Peer Gynt say: *One cannot die in the middle of Act Five* - funny because saying only fictional character.
- Gaarder uses romantic irony self-referentially (Alberto addressing Hilde/major) but philosophy point: author breaks illusion to reveal fictionality, echoing world-soul dreaming.

### Nationalism dark side as presented
- Text does not moralize heavily; presents strengthening national identity, harvesting folk culture as positive nourishing rediscovery. Implied danger only via Werther suicides, Satanic rebellion, dark/mystical preoccupation, God dark side, unrestrained ego. No explicit fascism discussion; focus organism idea applied to nation.
