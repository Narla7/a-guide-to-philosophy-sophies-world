# 26 - Cosmos and Finale - Big Bang and the White Rabbit

Context: Midnight glider talk at Bjerkely between Hilde and her father; cosmology coda while Sophie/Alberto invisibly listen (wrench, rowboat intrusions skipped).

## 1. Scale: looking out is looking back
- Strange to live on tiny planet; Earth one of many orbiting sun, yet only living planet — perhaps only one in entire universe, or universe teeming; inconceivably huge, measured in light-minutes/years.
- **Light-minute**: distance light travels in minute at 300,000 km/s = 60×300,000 = **18 million km**. Light-year ≈ **ten trillion km**.
- Sun a little over **8 light-minutes** away: June-day warmth traveled 8 minutes before reaching faces.
- Pluto, then outermost planet: ~**5 light-hours**; astronomer sees 5 hours back; picture takes 5 hours to get here.
- Thunder analogy: always hear thunder after lightning because sound slower than light; hearing thunder = hearing past event. Stars likewise: seeing star thousands of ly away = seeing "peal of thunder" from thousands of years ago. Everything seen meets eye as light waves taking time.
- Milky Way: own sun one of **400 billion** stars; large discus, sun in spiral arm; clear winter night broad band = looking toward center. Swedish "Winter Street."
- Nearest star: **4 light-years** (just above island); stargazer there with telescope pointing at Bjerkely would see 4 years ago — 11-year-old girl swinging legs in glider.
- Whole galaxy **90,000 light-years** wide; star 50,000 ly away = 50,000 years back.
- Beyond: ~**hundred billion galaxies** × ~hundred billion stars each. Nearest, Andromeda, **2 million ly**: looking 2M years back; lucky stargazer there pointing at Earth would not see us, at best flat-faced Neanderthals.
- Farthest then known: ~**10 billion ly**; signals chart universe twice as long as solar system has existed.
- Punchline repeated: can never know universe as **now**, only as **then**. "In a way, we are going home."

> "The only way we can look out into space, then, is to look back in time."

## 2. Expansion and the Big Bang
- Greater significance than distances: no galaxy stays put; all moving apart at colossal speeds, farther = quicker; distances increasing.
- Balloon with black spots: spots recede as balloon blown up = **universe expanding**.
- Agreed explanation: ~**15 billion years** ago all substance assembled in relatively small area, so dense gravity made it terrifically hot, finally exploded — **Big Bang**.
- Explosion expelled substance all directions; cooling formed stars/galaxies/moons/planets.
- Expanding precisely because of that explosion billions of years ago. Universe has no timeless geography; **universe is a happening, an explosion**.
- Future hinges on gravity vs expansion — same pair (gravity + inertia) Alberto used for planets orbiting sun:
  - Possibility A: expand forever, galaxies further apart.
  - Possibility B: in couple billion years gravity wins as explosive force weakens → packed together again, reverse explosion / **implosion**, like movie in slow motion / air released from balloon, all galaxies drawn into tight nucleus → another Big Bang, same laws, new stars/galaxies.
- Which depends how heavy/massive universe is — then unknown.
- If heavy enough to contract, obvious conclusion it may have expanded/contracted many times; theory divided: may be one-time only. If eternal expansion, where did stuff that exploded come from becomes even more pressing.

*Note: figures are ~1990 textbook cosmology (15B years, 100B×100B); keep faithful, do not update to 13.8B/200B+ in summary.*

## 3. Linear vs cyclic readings
- Christian: natural to see Big Bang as creation moment; Bible "Let there be light!" Christianity's **linear** history fits better with continuing expansion.
- Orient **cyclic** history, eternally repeating: India — world continually unfolds/folds, alternating **Brahman's Day / Brahman's Night**. Harmonizes with expand-contract-expand eternal cycle. Mental picture: great cosmic heart beating.
- Both equally inconceivable/exciting.
- Returns to Sophie's garden paradox: either universe always there — or suddenly came from nothing.

## 4. Stardust and the stone
- Phosphorescence/organisms made of elements once blended in a star. Hilde: "Us too?" Answer: **"Yes, we too are stardust."**
- Radio telescopes picking up light billions of ly away chart primeval post-Bang universe as cosmic fossils; astrologer can only "predict the past"; constellations already considerably different from 2,000 years ago; stars moved long before light reached us.
- You and I also began with Big Bang: all substance organic unity; once primeval age all matter in clump so massive pinhead weighed many billions of tons — **"primeval atom"** exploded from enormous gravitation, as if something disintegrated. Looking at sky = trying to find way back to ourselves.
- All stars/galaxies same substance, same family, lumped here/there across billions of ly, same origin.
- Big question: what is earthly substance? What exploded billions of years ago? Where from? Concerns us deeply: we are spark from great fire ignited billions of years ago.
- Anti-exaggeration: need not numbers; hold stone in hand — universe equally incomprehensible if only that orange-sized stone existed. Question equally impenetrable: where did this stone come from?
- Closing images (philosophical): sitting at very tip of fine hairs in white rabbit's fur; "planet is alive"; stars soon on water; "is anyone out there in night of light-years?" — wonder, not answer, is point.

> "When we look up at the sky, we are trying to find the way back to ourselves."
> "We are a spark from the great fire that was ignited many billions of years ago."
