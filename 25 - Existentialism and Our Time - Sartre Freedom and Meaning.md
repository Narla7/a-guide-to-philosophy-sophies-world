# 25 - Existentialism and Our Time - Sartre Freedom and Meaning

Context: Noon meeting in newly opened Cafe Pierre on Main Square; capstone "our century" lesson before garden-party story takes over.

## 1. Map of our century given here
- Existentialism = collective term for currents taking man's **existential situation** as point of departure; 20th-c. existential philosophy; roots via Kierkegaard, Hegel, Marx.
- Important precursor: Friedrich Nietzsche (1844–1900): reacted against Hegel/historicism, proposed **life itself** as counterweight to anemic history-interest and Christian "slave morality"; "revaluation of all values" so strongest's life-force not hampered by weak; Christianity/traditional philosophy turned away from real world toward heaven/world of ideas — hitherto "real" world in fact pseudo-world. Injunction: **"Be true to the world. Do not listen to those who offer you supernatural expectations."**
- Martin Heidegger (German): influenced by Kierkegaard + Nietzsche; named but not expounded.
- Focus: French **Jean-Paul Sartre (1905–1980)**, leading light to broader public, especially popular in 1940s just after war; later allied with Marxist movement in France, never party member. Met lifelong companion **Simone de Beauvoir**, also existential philosopher, in a cafe — hence cafe setting.
- Other 20th-c. renewals listed rapid-fire (see §6); existential questions declared unanswerable once-for-all — each generation/individual must re-ask.

## 2. Sartre: existence precedes essence
- Slogan glossed: **"existentialism is humanism"** — starts from nothing but humanity itself; bleaker than Renaissance humanism.
- Both Kierkegaard and some modern existentialists Christian; **Sartre atheistic**: merciless analysis when **"God is dead"** (phrase from Nietzsche).
- Key word **existence** ≠ merely being alive. Plants/animals alive/exist but need not think what it implies. Man alone conscious of own existence.
  - Material thing simply **"in itself"**; mankind **"for itself."** Being of man ≠ being of things.
- Formula: **"Existence takes priority over essence."**
  - Essence = what something consists of, nature/being.
  - Man has **no innate nature**; must create self, create own essence, because not fixed in advance.
  - Vain through whole history to seek what man/human nature is; useless to search for meaning of life in general.
  - **Condemned to improvise**: like actors dragged onto stage without learned lines, no script, no prompter whispering directions. Must decide how to live. Bible/philosophy book cannot supply practical answer.
- Consequence: when realize alive, will die, no meaning to cling to → **angst** (Kierkegaard's term retained).
- **Alienation** language from Hegel/Marx: feeling alien in meaningless world creates despair, boredom, nausea, absurdity. Described as 20th-c. city dweller's state; normal to feel depressed / everything too boring.

> "The fact that I exist takes priority over what I am."

## 3. Condemned to be free, responsibility, anti-bad-faith
- Renaissance humanists triumphantly stressed freedom/independence; Sartre experiences freedom as **curse**:
> "Man is condemned to be free. Condemned because he has not created himself — and is nevertheless free. Because having once been hurled into the world, he is responsible for everything he does."

- We did not ask to be created free — precisely Sartre's point — yet are free, condemned to choose throughout life.
- No eternal values/norms to adhere to → choices even more significant; totally responsible for everything we do.
- Forbidden moves:
  - Disclaim responsibility because "must" go to work, "must" live up to middle-class expectations.
  - Slip into anonymous masses → only members of impersonal flock, fled from self into **self-deception**.
- Positive demand: freedom obliges to make something of self, live **"authentically" / "truly."**
- Ethics especially: never lay blame on "human nature," "human frailty." Grown men behaving like pigs blaming "old Adam": **there is no old Adam**, merely figure clutched to avoid responsibility.
- Sophie's objection — ought to be limit to blame — left standing; Sartre's rigor presented without softening.
- **Not nihilism**: nihilist = nothing means anything, everything permissible. Sartre: life **must** have meaning, imperative; **we ourselves must create it**. "To exist is to create your own life."

## 4. Consciousness, perception, the absent Pierre
- Sartre's proof-attempt: consciousness in itself is **nothing until it has perceived something**; consciousness always conscious **of** something; "something" provided as much by ourselves as surroundings; partly decide what we perceive by selecting what is significant.
- Examples:
  - Two people same room experience quite differently; contribute own meaning/interests.
  - Pregnant woman thinks she sees pregnant women everywhere — not because none before, but now sees through different eyes.
  - Escaped convict sees policemen everywhere.
  - What is of no interest, don't see.
- Staged demonstration: Alberto deliberately 15 min late ("academic quarter").
  - Sophie's first notice: **that Alberto was absent**. Strange to notice what is absent, but she was to meet him.
  - Sartre uses just such cafe visit to show we **"annihilate"** irrelevant.
  - Lovers' analogues: waiting for loved one's call, "hear" him **not calling** all evening; arrange station meeting, crowds milling, cannot see him — all in way, unimportant, aggravating, taking too much room; only register **he is not there**.

## 5. Simone de Beauvoir and feminism
- Applies existentialism: as Sartre says man has no basic nature, creates self — also true of sexes.
- Received view she rejects: man "transcending"/achieving, seeking meaning outside home; woman "immanent," wishing to be where she is, nurturing family, environment, homely "feminine values."
- De Beauvoir **denied** any such female/male nature; men/women must liberate from ingrown prejudices/ideals.
- Main work *The Second Sex* (1949): women treated as second sex; men behave as subjects, treat women as objects, depriving them of responsibility for own life.
- Gloss offered: women exactly as free/independent as choose to be.

## 6. Absurdism / theater of the absurd
- Great influence on literature 1940s–present, especially drama. Sartre wrote plays/novels. Others: French **Albert Camus**, Irish **Samuel Beckett**, Romanian **Eugène Ionesco**, Polish **Witold Gombrowicz**. Characteristic style = **absurdism**, esp. "theater of the absurd."
- Absurd = meaningless/irrational.
- Aim paradox: show lack of meaning to get audience to **disagree**; not cultivate meaningless; by exposing absurd in ordinary situations force onlookers to seek truer, more essential life.
- Devices:
  - Absolutely trivial situations → **hyperrealism**: reproduce exactly bathroom morning in ordinary home on stage → audience laughs; laughter interpretable as **defense mechanism** against seeing self lampooned.
  - Surrealistic/dreamlike situations accepted by characters **without surprise** → audience compelled to react in surprise at lack of surprise. Method compared to Chaplin silents: laconic acceptance of absurd compels audience to look inward for genuine/true.
- Existential maxim: at times right to feel must get away even without knowing where to go — if house catches fire must get out even with nowhere to live.

**Faithfulness note:** Camus's own philosophy of absurd/Sisyphus/suicide is **not** expounded; he appears only in writer-list. Do not import it.

## 7. Other 20th-century currents (as listed, not developed)
- **Neo-Thomism**: Aquinas tradition.
- **Analytical philosophy / logical empiricism**: roots in Hume/British empiricism, even Aristotle's logic.
- **Neo-Marxism** in myriad trends.
- Already covered: Neo-Darwinism, psychoanalysis.
- **Materialism**: historical roots; current science traced to pre-Socratics; search for indivisible elemental particle; no satisfactory account of "matter"; nuclear physics/biochemistry fascination vital life-philosophy for many.
- Old/new jumbled: very questions course started with still unanswered.
- Sartre observation: existential questions cannot be answered once-for-all.
- Counter-bleak note: by asking know we are alive; seeking ultimate answers yields clear/final solutions to other problems; science/research/technology as by-products of wonder. Wonder brought men to moon. Armstrong: "One small step for man, one giant leap for mankind" — summed drawing with him all who lived before; not his merit alone.

## 8. Ecophilosophy, New Age, skepticism, communication
### Ecophilosophy / ecosophy
- Most serious new problems = environment. Central direction: **ecophilosophy/ecosophy**, founder Norwegian **Arne Næss**.
- Warning: Western civilization on fundamentally wrong track, racing head-on into planet's tolerance limits; soundings deeper than concrete pollution/destruction; something basically wrong with Western thought.
- Example: questioned evolution's assumption man "at top" as masters of nature; fatal for living planet.
- Remedy sought in India, "primitive"/native peoples, e.g. Native Americans, to rediscover lost.
- Scientific "paradigm shift," holism, alternative movements/new lifestyle; must distinguish good/bad; not all new good, not all old to throw out — hence need for history to orient.

### New Age / occult market
- With dwindling Christianity, alternative philosophical market mushroomed: New Religion, New Occultism, superstition become industry.
- Bookstore wall: NEW AGE / ALTERNATIVE LIFESTYLES / MYSTICISM; titles *Life after Death?, Secrets of Spiritism, Tarot, UFO Phenomenon, Healing, Return of the Gods, You Have Been Here Before, What Is Astrology?* Hundreds + stacks.
- "Temple of our age"; sells as well as pornography; kind of pornography; difference real philosophy : these books :: real love : pornography.
- Alberto in park: ESP/parapsychology, telepathy, clairvoyance, psychokinesis, spiritism, astrology, "urology" [sic; ufology meant]. Largely humbug, detailed map of nonexistent landscape; Hume would commit to flames; many books not one iota genuine experience. Commercial enterprise; people desire mystical break from dreary monotony. "Carrying coals to Newcastle": already wandering wonderful adventure, creation emerging broad daylight — why fortune-teller tent/backyards of academe?
- Not all writers phonies/liars; Darwinian system again:
  - Collect coincidences: store 28 crowns, Joanna repays 28, cinema seat 28 → looks mysterious; collect strange experiences from billions into books → looks like data, grows; **lottery where only winning numbers visible**.
  - Exclude phonies → Freud's unconscious explains rest: serve as "mediums" for own unconscious; sleep-talking/walking as mental automatism; hypnosis not of own volition; surrealist automatic writing as medium for own unconscious.
  - Spiritualist revivals: medium contacts deceased via voice/automatic writing, taken as afterlife/reincarnation proof. Often good faith but only medium for own unconscious. Case: woman with no Hebrew passing Hebrew messages → had Jewish nanny as child; incredible storage capacity.
  - Everyday: sudden call from long-unheard friend just as looking for number — both heard same old song on radio last heard together; unaware of connection.
  - Rule: humbug, winning-number effect, or unconscious. Healthier skepticism. English skeptics' association offered large reward for slightest supernatural proof, tiny telepathy — nobody came forward.
- Openness without dogma: much not understood; magnetism/electricity once magic; great-grandmother wide-eyed at TV/computers. "Supernatural" curious term; believe only one nature, but absolutely astonishing. True philosophers keep eyes open for **white crow** though never seen; skeptic one day obliged to accept new phenomenon; otherwise dogmatic, not philosopher. Bookstore's flimsy PHILOSOPHY shelf holding *Sophie's World* = white Crow (story link, one line).

### Planetary civilization
- Window of TVs/VCRs/satellite/mobile/computers/fax: 20th c. Renaissance world exploded outward via voyages; today **explosion in reverse** — drawn together into one communications network. Once days by horse/carriage to meet philosophers; now anywhere access whole human experience on screen. Question whether history ending or threshold of new age; no longer city/country citizens but planetary civilization. Tech, esp. communications, more dramatic last 30–40 years than whole prior history; probably only beginning.
