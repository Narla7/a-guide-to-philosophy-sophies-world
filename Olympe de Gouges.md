# Olympe de Gouges

*Context: French Enlightenment seven-point program, Point 7 — Human rights. Revolution 1787/1789 established rights for "citizens," but citizen nearly always man. Yet Revolution gave first inklings of feminism.*

## 1. Condorcet

- **Condorcet**: as early as 1787 published treatise on rights of women; held women same natural rights as men.

## 2. Women in the 1789 Revolution

- During 1789 Revolution women extremely active against feudal regime; women led demonstrations forcing king from Versailles palace; women's groups formed Paris; demanded same political rights, plus marriage-law and social-condition changes.
- Result: did not get equal rights. As on many later occasions, women's rights exploited in heat of struggle, then old male-dominated society reintroduced once new regime settled — "Typical!"

## 3. Olympe de Gouges (1748–1793)

- Fought hardest; 1791 (2 years after revolution) published Declaration on Rights of Women; declaration on citizen lacked women's natural rights; demanded all same rights for women as men.
- **Beheaded 1793; all political activity for women banned.**
- Feminism really under way only 19th century, France and rest Europe; Norway women vote 1913; many parts world still fight.
- Encyclopedia censorship motif in-story: Hilde finds no Gouges in small encyclopedia ("Society for Protection of Men" joke), then in large family encyclopedia: "Gouges, Marie Olympe (1748–1793), Fr. author, prominent role during French Revolution with brochures on social questions and plays. One of few ... campaigned for human rights to apply to women. In 1791 published 'Declaration on the Rights of Women.' Beheaded 1793 for daring to defend Louis XVI and oppose Robespierre."

## 4. Wider rights context (Point 7)

- French more practical than English: not content with theory, fought actively for "natural rights" of citizen.
- First campaign against **censorship — freedom of press**. Also religion, morals, politics: individual's right to freedom of thought/utterance secured. Fought for abolition of slavery and more humane treatment of criminals.
- Principle of "inviolability of individual" culminated in **Declaration of the Rights of Man and Citizen** adopted by French National Assembly in 1789 (text typo "178V"), basis for Norway's Constitution 1814.
- **Natural rights**: certain rights everybody entitled simply by being born. Still invoked as "natural right" often in conflict with laws of land; individuals/whole nations claim it rebelling against anarchy, servitude, oppression.
- Slogan: **LIBERTY, EQUALITY AND FRATERNITY**.
