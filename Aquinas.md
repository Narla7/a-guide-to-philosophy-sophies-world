# Aquinas

*Context: St. Thomas Aquinas (1225–1274) — the High Scholastic who christianized Aristotle, building the great synthesis of faith and reason in natural theology, being, and morals.*

## 1. Life and Project — Christianizing Aristotle

**Thesis:** As Augustine christianized Plato, Aquinas christianizes Aristotle — interpreting him so as not to threaten dogma, creating the synthesis of faith and knowledge.

**Arguments / Details:**
- From Aquino between Rome and Naples; teacher at University of Paris.
- Philosopher and theologian (no sharp divide then).
- Teacher: Albert the Great.
- Context: ~400 years after Augustine; convent schools → cathedral schools (10–11 o'clock) → universities / cathedrals at noon (~1200s; St. Mary's itself High Gothic 1200s).
- Late-12th-century Arab scholars invited to Northern Italy; Aristotle retranslated Greek / Arabic → Latin; revived natural science; forced question: when to follow Aristotle, when the Bible?
- Project: "christianize" Aristotle as Augustine christianized Plato — enter Aristotle's philosophy and take him at his word, explain him so as not to threaten dogma.

**Terms:** synthesis of faith and knowledge; High Scholasticism.

**Legacy:** makes Aristotle compatible with Christianity; authorizes natural science within faith; shapes Catholic doctrine.

## 2. Faith vs. Reason — One Truth, Two Paths

**Thesis:** No necessary conflict between what reason / philosophy teaches and what revelation / faith teaches; we can often reason to the same truths the Bible states.

**Arguments:**
- Going only part of the way ≠ going the wrong way.
- Distinction: verities of faith (accessible only via belief / revelation — e.g. God created world in six days; Jesus is Son of God) vs. "natural theological truths" accessible both via faith and via innate / natural reason (e.g. that there is a God).
- Two paths to God: faith + revelation (surest; reason alone easily loses its way) and reason + senses. Overlap at significant points.
- Christendom and philosophy often say the same thing.
- Aristotle's "God" as formal cause setting natural processes going is accepted as far as he goes; fuller personal description only from Bible / Jesus.

**Examples:**
- Saying "Athens is in Europe" is true but imprecise; geography book adds capital of Greece, southeastern Europe, Acropolis, Socrates / Plato / Aristotle. Aristotle likewise true as far as he goes; lacks revelation's fuller truth.

**Terms:** natural theology vs. theology of faith; natural theological truths; formal cause.

## 3. Existence of God — Reason's Reach (Honest Note on the Book)

**Thesis:** Human reason cannot disprove God; further, God's existence can be proved on Aristotelian basis from reason — everything around us must have a "formal cause."

**Arguments:**
- Claim: human reason cannot disprove God (most would agree even today); Aquinas goes further — God's existence can be *proved* on Aristotelian basis, from reason: everything around us must have a "formal cause."
- Honest note on Gaarder's presentation in this chapter: Gaarder asserts this proof-project and grounds it in formal causality and natural theology, but in this passage does **not** enumerate the famous Five Ways individually. No list of motion / causation / contingency / degrees / teleology is given here; the operative argument presented is the formal-cause / reason-plus-senses argument summarized above.

**Examples / Analogies:**
- Thunderstorm: thunder heard though blind, lightning seen though deaf; best to both see and hear; impressions reinforce, not contradict.
- Steinbeck's *Of Mice and Men*: reading the creation reveals something of the creator (cares about outsiders; a person wrote it) but not biographical particulars (age, residence, children when written). For that need biography / autobiography. Likewise: walking in nature reveals *that* God is and that He loves flowers / animals; *who* God is as person only in Bible, God's "autobiography."

**Terms:** formal cause; natural theology.

## 4. Ethics — Two Paths to the Moral Life / Natural Law

**Thesis:** Both a revealed morality and a natural morality from conscience; Bible surest, but natural conscience parallels it.

**Arguments:**
- Bible teaches how God wants us to live; God also gives conscience to distinguish right / wrong on a "natural" basis.
- Hence both a "theology of faith" and "natural theology," both a revealed and a natural morality.

**Examples:**
- We know harming people is wrong even without reading "do unto others as you would have them do unto you."

**Terms:** revealed morality; natural morality / natural law; conscience.

## 5. Synthesis in Logic, Knowledge, Nature — The Scale of Being

**Thesis:** Adopt Aristotelian logic, theory of knowledge, and natural philosophy wherever not colliding with theology, culminating in a graded ladder of being to God.

**Arguments:**
- Adopts Aristotelian logic, theory of knowledge, natural philosophy wherever not colliding with theology.
- Example: Aristotle's progressive scale plants → animals → humans, indicating God as maximum of existence, aligned with Christian theology.
- Aquinas's ladder: plants / animals → man → angels → God.
  - Man like animals: body + senses; plus intelligence to reason things out stepwise ("ponder," infer conclusions).
  - Angels: no body / senses; spontaneous immediate intelligence; no need to learn stepwise; know without discursive reasoning; created (not everlasting like God) yet deathless because no body to depart from.
  - God above angels: sees / knows everything in one single coherent vision; timeless — our "now" is not God's "now"; weeks passing for us need not pass for God.

**Terms:** scale of being; timeless divine vision; discursive vs. immediate intelligence.

## 6. Women — The Book's Critical Presentation

**Thesis:** Gaarder presents Aquinas as adopting Aristotle's inferiority view, with a "cold comfort" spiritual-equality qualifier, then critiques it.

**Arguments / Details:**
- Adopts Aristotle's view: woman as more or less incomplete man; only father transmits characteristics (man active / creative, woman passive / receptive); harmonized with Bible (woman from Adam's rib).
- Gaarder's critique ("Nonsense!"): notes mammal ova undiscovered until 1827, making the male-only creative force less surprising then.
- Qualifier reported: only as *nature-being* is woman inferior; woman's soul equals man's; in Heaven full sexual equality because physical gender differences cease. Presented as cold comfort.
- Counter-voice: medieval church male-dominated, but women thinkers existed — e.g. Hildegard of Bingen (see [[Hildegard of Bingen]]).

**Terms:** active / passive; nature-being vs. soul equality.

## 7. Terms, Quotes, Legacy

**Terms:** synthesis of faith and knowledge; natural theology vs. theology of faith; natural theological / moral truths; formal cause; scale of being; timeless divine vision.
**Quotes / Analogies:** Athens / Europe; thunder / lightning; Steinbeck / autobiography.
**Legacy:** makes Aristotle compatible with Christianity; authorizes natural science within faith; systematizes High Scholasticism; shapes Catholic doctrine on God, soul, angels, natural law.
