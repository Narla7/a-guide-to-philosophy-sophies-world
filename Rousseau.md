# Rousseau

*Context: French Enlightenment, mid-18th century alongside [[Voltaire]] and [[Montesquieu]]. Dating anchors: Hume died 1776, only 2 years before Voltaire and Rousseau (both 1778). All three had been to England, knew Locke. Rousseau is the "back to nature" philosopher of the seven-point program, and precursor (via feeling) to [[19 - Romanticism - Feeling Nature and Genius|Romanticism]].*

## 1. Back to nature

- Catchphrase "back to nature." Nature to Enlightenment = almost same as **reason**, since human reason gift of nature rather than religion/civilization.
- Observation: so-called primitive peoples frequently healthier/happier than Europeans because not "civilized."
- **Rousseau** proposes "We should return to nature." Nature good; man "by nature" good; civilization ruins him.
- Criticism of "civilization" already voiced by French Enlightenment philosophers — "Maybe we should have listened."

## 2. Education and childhood

- Child should remain in naturally innocent state as long as possible.
- Idea of **intrinsic value of childhood** dates from Enlightenment. Previously childhood merely preparation for adult life. But we are human beings living life on earth even as children.
- Great attention to education of children and people; science of **pedagogy founded** during Enlightenment. (Schools date Middle Ages, pedagogy Enlightenment.)

## 3. Shared seven-point program

1. Opposition to authority (clergy, king, nobility; English example: Newton, Locke; Descartes ground-up skepticism)
2. Rationalism / Age of Reason (Locke inconsistent empiricist; French "evident" vs British "common sense"; Socrates/Stoics parallel)
3. Enlightenment movement and Encyclopedia (28 volumes, 1751–1772, "needles to cannons")
4. Cultural optimism (reason/knowledge → progress)
5. Return to nature (this file's core)
6. Natural religion / Deism (Supreme Being revealing only through nature/natural laws)
7. Human rights (press, religion, morals, politics; abolition of slavery; humane criminals; Declaration 1789; Norway's Constitution 1814) — women's-rights struggle detailed in [[Olympe de Gouges]].

- Slogan: **LIBERTY, EQUALITY AND FRATERNITY** / Family of Man letter.
- Later Romantic echo outside chunk, but within chunk this is Rousseau core presented.
