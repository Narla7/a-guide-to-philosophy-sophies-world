# 24 - Freud - Unconscious Dreams and Repression

Context: One late-afternoon lesson in Alberto's cabin; almost pure theory with only Emperor's-nakedness joke as lead-in.

## 1. Who Freud is in this book
- Sigmund Freud (1856–), Vienna medicine, neurology specialist, lived through flourishing Viennese culture.
- Toward close of 19th century and far into "our own" developed **depth psychology / psychoanalysis**.
- Defined as both general description of mind **and** therapy for nervous/mental disorders. Alberto refuses complete survey; goal is only what is needed to understand what a human being is.
- Placed as exponent of late-19th-century **naturalistic currents**: man in constant tension/conflict between drives/needs and demands of society.
- "No exaggeration to say Freud discovered human drives."
- Polemic against 18th-century rationalism: man is **not** so rational; irrational impulses often determine thought, dream, action. Example: sexual drive as basic as baby's instinct to suckle.
- What was new was not drives as such but **disguise / sublimation**: basic needs steer us without our awareness.
- Empirical basis stressed: discovered children's sexuality and childhood-conflict origin of neuroses **in psychotherapy practice**, not armchair speculation. Hence "archeology of the soul" therapy.

> "Psychoanalysis is a description of the human mind in general as well as a therapy for nervous and mental disorders."
> "Man is not really such a rational creature as the eighteenth-century rationalists liked to think."

## 2. Id – Ego – Superego in full
### Id = pleasure principle
- Newborn lives physical/mental needs directly, unashamedly: cries if no milk, wet diaper; direct demand for contact, bodily warmth.
- Freud calls this **id**. As newborns "we are hardly anything but id."
- Id is carried into adulthood throughout life.

### Ego = reality principle, regulative
- Gradually learn to regulate desires, adjust to surroundings.
- Develop an **ego** with regulative function: even if we want/need something, cannot lie down and scream till we get it.
- Outside world will not accept some desires → **repress**: push away, try to forget.

### Superego = internalized moral demands
- From infancy faced with parents/society: "Don't do that!" "Naughty naughty, that's bad!"
- Even grown up, echo remains; world's moral expectations become part of us.
- **Conscience is a component** of superego, but superego goes further: tells us when **desires themselves** are bad/improper, not least erotic/sexual desire.
- Infantile sexuality example, very concrete:
  - Infants like touching sex organs; observable on any beach.
  - In Freud's Vienna: 2–3-year-old gets slap over fingers + "Naughty!" / "Don't do that!" / "Keep your hands on top of the covers!"
  - Beginning of guilt about sex organs/sexuality; remains in superego; most people feel guilty about sex all life, per Freud.
  - Yet sexual desires/needs are natural and vital → **lifelong conflict between desire and guilt** is set.
- Sophie asks if conflict died down since Freud. Alberto: "Most certainly." But neuroses still illustrate mechanism.

### Clinical illustration: the brother-in-law case
- Woman patient secretly in love with brother-in-law. Sister dies → thought flashes: "Now he is free to marry me!"
- On frontal collision course with superego; so monstrous she **immediately repressed** it, buried in unconscious.
- Quote from Freud reproduced: young girl ill with severe hysterical symptoms, had thoroughly forgotten scene at sister's bedside and "odious egoistic impulse," remembered in great agitation during analysis, reproduced pathogenic moment, cured.
- This is what "archeology" means:
  - Archeologist: knife from 18th c., comb from 14th c. deeper, urn from 5th c. B.C. deeper still.
  - Analyst with patient's help digs into mind, brings traumatic experience into conscious, holds it up, helps patient "be done with it."

## 3. Topography: conscious – preconscious – unconscious
- After years of treatment Freud concluded conscious is only small part; **tip of iceberg above sea level**; below threshold is subconscious/unconscious.
- Distinction insisted on:
  - **Preconscious**: thought/experienced but not presently conscious; recallable if we "put our mind to it."
  - **Unconscious**: what we have **repressed** because unpleasant, improper, nasty. Superego shoves intolerable desires/urges "downstairs. Away with them!"
- Mechanism in all healthy people, but keeping forbidden thoughts from consciousness can cost so much strain it produces mental illness.
- Repressed tries of its own accord to re-enter consciousness; takes great effort to keep under critical eye.
- 1909 America lecture illustration quoted at length:
  - Disturber laughing/talking/scraping feet in hall distracts lecturer; strong men eject him → **repressed**, lecture continues.
  - To prevent return, men take chairs at door as **resistance** to keep up repression.
  - Transfer locations: inside = consciousness, outside = unconscious. "Tolerably good illustration."
- Upshot: under constant pressure from below; often say/do things without intending; unconscious prompts feelings/actions.

## 4. Defense mechanisms in everyday life
### Parapraxes / slips
- Slips of tongue or pen: accidentally say/do what was repressed.
- Foreman toasting unpopular boss (in plain words "a swine"): stands, raises glass, says **"Here's to the swine!"** Said only what he really meant, but did not mean to say it.
- Bishop with unusually big nose coming to tea; minister's well-behaved daughters instructed on no account refer to nose. Children blurt because repressive mechanism not yet developed. Strain not to look/forget, thinking about it whole time. Asked to pass sugar: **"Do you take sugar in your nose?"**

### Rationalization
- Not giving real reason to self or others because real reason unacceptable.
- Hypnosis example: hypnotized to open window when Alberto drums fingers; drums → opens; asked why → "too hot." Reluctant to admit obeyed hypnotic order → rationalizes.
- Sophie's 4-year-old cousin happy when she visits; she says must hurry home to mom; he says "she's stupid!" Means: stupid she must go, too shy to say so.
- "We all encounter that sort of thing practically every day."

### Projection
- Transfer characteristics repressed in self onto others.
- Very miserly person characterizes others as penny-pinchers.
- One who will not admit sex-preoccupation first to be incensed at others' sex-fixation.

### General bungled actions
- Forgetting name, fumbling clothes while talking, shifting random objects, stumbling over words.
- Freud's point: neither as accidental nor innocent as we think; can reveal most intimate secrets.
- Health advice: art is precisely **not** to expend too much effort burying unpleasant in unconscious. Like blocking water-vole nest entrance: vole pops up elsewhere. Healthy to leave door ajar. Lock it → sick.
- **Neurotic**: uses too much energy keeping unpleasant out; frequently particular experience desperately repressed; yet anxious for doctor to help find way back to hidden traumas.

## 5. Therapy: free association
- Patient lies relaxed, talks about whatever comes to mind — however irrelevant, random, unpleasant, embarrassing.
- Idea: break through "lid" / "control" grown over traumas; traumas active all the time, just not consciously.
- Paradox: harder you try to forget, more you think about it unconsciously → important to heed signals from unconscious.

## 6. Dreams: royal road, manifest vs latent, dream-work
- *The Interpretation of Dreams* (1900), after years with patients and self-analysis: dreams **not random**; unconscious tries to communicate with conscious.
- Thesis: **all dreams are wish-fulfillments**.
  - Clearly observable in children: dream ice cream, cherries.
  - Adults: wishes disguised because **censorship/repression still works in sleep**, though weaker than waking, still strong enough to distort unacknowledgeable wishes.
- Hence dreams must be interpreted.
- Distinction:
  - **Manifest dream**: actual dream as recalled morning; "film"/"video"; scenario always takes material from previous day.
  - **Latent dream thoughts**: deeper hidden meaning, may stem from distant past, earliest childhood.
- Interpretation must be done **with therapist but not by therapist alone**; doctor as Socratic "midwife."
- **Dream-work**: process converting latent thoughts to manifest aspect; masking/coding what dream really about. Interpretation reverses: unmask/decode motif to theme.
- Worked example constructed in dialogue:
  - Young man dreams female cousin gives him **two balloons**.
  - Manifest: gets balloons; scenario from previous day — fair, newspaper picture, even word "balloon" or reminder suffices.
  - Naive reading "wanted balloons" rejected: young man would hardly ardently wish for balloons; if he had, no need to dream.
  - Freudian reading: really wants cousin; balloons = her breasts; presupposes wish felt as embarrassment.
- Formula: dream is **"disguised fulfillment of a repressed wish."**
- Historical note: exactly what is repressed may have changed considerably since Vienna doctor days; mechanism of disguised content still intact.

## 7. Freud, art, literature, inspiration
- Psychoanalysis extremely important in 1920s for certain psychiatric patients; theory of unconscious also very significant for art/literature.
- Interest in unconscious already predominant in literature in last decade of 19th c. **before** psychoanalysis known → appearance in 1890s no coincidence, "in spirit of times."
- Freud did **not** claim to discover repression, defense mechanisms, rationalizing; first to apply to psychiatry; master at literary illustration.
- From 1920s direct influence: poets/painters, especially **surrealists**, exploit unconscious.
  - Surrealism = French "super realism." André Breton's 1924 surrealistic manifesto: art from unconscious, freest inspiration from dream images, dissolve dream/reality boundary, break conscious censorship, let words/images have free play.
- "Freud demonstrated there is an artist in everyone." Dream = little work of art, new every night. Interpreting dreams requires working through dense symbols like picture/literary text.
- Everyone dreams ~20% of sleep, 1–2 hours nightly; disturbed in dream phases → nervous, irritable. Innate need to give artistic expression to existential situation. We are directors, set scenario, play all roles. One who says doesn't understand art doesn't know self well.
- Evidence of mind's wonders: retain everything seen/experienced deep in consciousness; can be brought to light. Memory lapse → "tip of tongue" → suddenly remember = lay in unconscious, slips through half-open door.
- Inspiration: doors/drawers fly open, words/images tumble out, as if from outside source. Observable in overtired children suddenly telling story with seemingly unlearned words/ideas; words/ideas lay latent, surfacing when caution/censorship lets go. Important not to let reason/reflection control unconscious expression.
- **Centipede and tortoise** story (serious/sad):
  - Centipede amazing dancer with 100 legs; forest impressed; tortoise dislikes, cannot say so or claim better, devises fiendish plan.
  - Letter: "O incomparable centipede... Is it left leg 28 then right leg 39? Or right 17 before left 44? Await answer..." Centipede begins thinking which leg first/next → never dances again.
  - Moral: imagination strangled by reasoned deliberation.
- Surrealist **automatic writing**: white paper, write without thinking what is written; term from spiritualism where medium believes departed spirit guides pen (more tomorrow).
- Surrealist as medium — but medium of **own unconscious**.
- Creativity = delicate interplay imagination/reason:
  - Too frequent: reason throttles imagination; without imagination nothing really new.
  - Imagination like Darwinian system: mutants arise one after another, only few get right to live; masses of ideas occur if refrain from censoring; only some usable. Reason has vital selective function. Day's catch laid on table must be selected.
  - If everything striking us passed lips/notepads/drawers, world would sink under casual impulses, no selection.
  - Imagination creates new, does not select/compose. Composition — every work of art is one — created in wondrous interplay mind/reflection, always element of chance. "Turn sheep loose before herd them."
  - Philosophers too need bold letting-go to think new thoughts **plus** reflection (irony: Disney flock appears just as Alberto says this — blamed on major, his sheep).

- Only contextual resistance recorded: respectable middle-class Viennese reacted with abhorrence to "sexuality of the child," made him very unpopular; "Victorianism" when sexuality taboo.
- Only historicizing qualification: repressed contents change; mechanism remains.
