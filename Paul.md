# Paul

*Split from [[09 - Two Cultures - Indo-Europeans Semites Jesus Paul|09 - Two Cultures]] §6–§7 — full detail preserved. Overview kept in source file.*

## 1. Resurrection foundation
- Days after crucified/buried, rumors risen from grave — proved no ordinary man, truly "Son of God."
- Church founded Easter Morning.
- Paul: **"And if Christ be not risen, then is our preaching vain and your faith is also vain."**

## 2. Resurrection of body vs immortal soul
- Now mankind can hope resurrection of body; crucified to save us.
- Jewish view: no "immortality of soul" or "transmigration" — that is Greek/Indo-European thought.
- Christianity: nothing in man — no "soul" — in itself immortal. Church believes "resurrection of the body and eternal life" by God's miracle saving from death/"damnation" — neither own merit nor natural/innate ability.

## 3. Glad tidings
- Early Christians preach salvation through faith in Jesus Christ; mediation makes Kingdom about reality; entire world can be won for Christ ("christ" = Greek translation of Hebrew "messiah," anointed one).

## 4. Paul's mission — congregations
- Few years after death, Pharisee Paul converts; many missionary journeys across Greco-Roman world make it worldwide religion (Acts of Apostles; Epistles to congregations).
- Decades after death congregations in Athens, Rome, Alexandria, Ephesos, Corinth; in 300–400 years entire Hellenistic world Christian.

## 5. Paul in Athens (Areopagos) — full speech
- Wanders into marketplace/philosophic capital; spirit stirred seeing city wholly idolatry.
- Visits synagogue, converses Epicurean/Stoic philosophers.
- Taken to Areopagos hill: *"May we know what this new doctrine... thou bringest certain strange things to our ears..."*
- Jew speaking of crucified/risen savior — coming collision Greek philosophy vs redemption — yet Athenians listen.
- Beneath Acropolis temples speech (Acts):
  > "Ye men of Athens, I perceive that in all things ye are too superstitious. For as I passed by, and beheld your devotions, I found an altar with this inscription, TO THE UNKNOWN GOD. Whom therefore ye ignorantly worship, him declare I unto you. God that made the world and all things therein, seeing that he is Lord of heaven and earth, dwelleth not in temples made with hands; neither is worshipped with men's hands, as though he needed any thing, seeing he giveth to all life, and breath, and all things... that they should seek the Lord... For in him we live, and move, and have our being; as certain also of your own poets have said, For we are also his offspring... we ought not to think that the Godhead is like unto gold, or silver, or stone, graven by art and man's device... but now commandeth all men everywhere to repent: Because he hath appointed a day, in the which he will judge the world in righteousness by that man whom he hath ordained; whereof he hath given assurance unto all men, in that he hath raised him from the dead."

## 6. Interpretation — collision and common ground
- Christianity penetrates as something else, completely different from Epicurean/Stoic/Neoplatonic philosophy.
- Yet common ground: search for God natural to all — not new to Greeks.
- New: God revealed himself, reached out — no longer "philosophic God" approachable by understanding, nor image gold/silver/stone (plenty on Acropolis/marketplace).
- Personal God intervening in history, dying on Cross for mankind.

## 7. Response
- Some mock resurrection; others "We will hear thee again"; some follow/believe — notably woman **Damaris** (women among most fervent converts).
- Mission continues.

## 8. Paul as organizer — Old -> New Covenant
- Not only missionary but guidance within congregations needing spiritual direction.
- Universalism question: Could non-Jews become Christians without first becoming Jews — e.g. Greek observe dietary laws? Paul: unnecessary.
- Christianity more than Jewish sect; universal salvation message. **"Old Covenant" God-Israel replaced by "New Covenant" Jesus established God-mankind.**

## 9. Need for dogma — Creed context
- Not only religion then; Hellenistic fusion required church concise summary to distance from other religions and prevent schisms → first **Creed** summing central dogmas/tenets.
- Central tenet — fully God, fully man: Jesus both God and man. Not "Son of God" by actions alone. God himself, but also "true man" sharing misfortunes, actually suffering on Cross. Sounds contradiction, but message precisely **God became man**. Not "demigod" half-man/half-god (widespread Greek/Hellenistic belief). Formula: **"perfect God, perfect man."**
