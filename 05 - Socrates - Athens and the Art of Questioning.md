# 05 - Socrates - Athens and the Art of Questioning

*Chapter context: After the pre-Socratics, philosophy moves to Athens (~450 BC onward) with Socrates (470-399 BC), Plato and Aristotle.*

## 1. Historical Thesis: A New Era and a New Project
- **Temporal/geographical break:** Socrates is the first great philosopher born in Athens; he and his successors lived/worked there. Natural philosophers = **pre-Socratics** (even Democritus, though he died after Socrates, belongs intellectually to pre-Socratic natural philosophy).
- **Athens after ~450 BC:** cultural centre of Greek world; democracy with popular assemblies and courts develops.
- **Shift in philosophical project:** from nature/physical world (history of science) to **individual and individual's place in society**, life, ethics, good and evil.
  - Cicero (Roman, centuries later) on Socrates: he "called philosophy down from the sky and established her in the towns and introduced her into homes and forced her to investigate life, ethics, good and evil."
- **Democratic precondition:** for democracy to work people must be educated; must master **rhetoric** = saying things convincingly. Modern parallel Gaarder draws: young democracy needs popular enlightenment.

## 2. The Opponents/Background: The Sophists
- **Who:** itinerant teachers/philosophers from Greek colonies flocking to Athens, teaching citizens **for money**.
- **Word:** "sophist" = wise and informed person.
- **Common with natural philosophers:** critical of traditional mythology.
- **Break:** rejected fruitless speculation about nature/universe; **skepticism** = view that although answers may exist, man cannot know truth about riddles of nature/universe.
- **Turn to man:** even if we cannot know nature's riddles, people must learn to live together; concern = man and his place in society.
- **Protagoras (c.485-410 BC):** "Man is the measure of all things" = right/wrong, good/bad must always be considered in relation to person's needs.
  - On gods: "The question is complex and life is short." One who cannot say categorically whether gods/God exist = **agnostic**.
- **Travel → relativism/social criticism:** had seen different governments, conventions, local laws varied widely → raised **natural vs socially induced** question.
  - Example: "natural modesty" — is fear of showing oneself naked innate (born with) or socially induced? Answer from traveller: not natural/innate, first and foremost social convention.
- **Effect:** bitter wrangling in Athens by pointing out no absolute norms for right/wrong.
- **Socrates' counter-thesis:** some such norms ARE absolute and universally valid.

## 3. Who Was Socrates? The Enigma
- **Dates:** 470-399 BC.
- **No writings:** never wrote a single line, yet among greatest influence on European thought, partly due to dramatic death.
- **Life:** born Athens, spent most life in city squares/marketplaces talking with people met there. Quote: "The trees in the countryside can teach me nothing." Could stand lost in thought for hours.
- **Appearance/character:** extremely ugly — potbellied, bulging eyes, snub nose — but inside "perfectly delightful." Saying: "You can seek him in the present, you can seek him in the past, but you will never find his equal."
- **Enigmatic founder:** considered enigmatic even in lifetime; soon after death held as founder of many different schools; very ambiguity allowed widely differing schools to claim him.
- **Source problem:** life known mainly through Plato, pupil and greatest philosopher, who wrote **Dialogues** — dramatized discussions using Socrates as principal character/mouthpiece.
  - Since Plato puts own philosophy in Socrates' mouth, cannot be sure words were ever uttered by him. Cannot easily distinguish Socrates' teachings vs Plato's philosophy.
  - Parallel: historical Jesus — cannot be certain historical Jesus spoke words Matthew/Luke ascribe. What historical Socrates actually said shrouded in mystery.
  - Gaarder's point: who Socrates "really" was relatively unimportant; **Plato's portrait** inspired Western world for ~2,500 years.

## 4. The Art of Discourse: Midwife, Irony, Gadfly
- **Not instructing but discussing:** did not appear to want to instruct; gave impression of desiring to learn from interlocutors. Instead of lecturing like traditional schoolmaster, discussed.
- **Questioning to refutation:** just asked questions, especially to begin as if knew nothing; in course of discussion got opponents to recognize weakness of arguments, forced into corner, finally obliged to realize what was right/wrong.
- **Midwife art (maieutics):** mother was midwife; his art like hers — she does not herself give birth but helps during delivery. Similarly helps people "give birth" to correct insight, since real understanding must come from within, cannot be imparted by someone else. Only understanding from within leads to true insight.
  - Precise formulation: ability to give birth is natural; everybody can grasp philosophical truths if just use **innate reason** = reaching down inside and using what is there.
  - Applied egalitarianism: held slave had same common sense as man of rank; would buttonhole slave with olive oil jar and ask philosophy.
- **Socratic irony:** by playing ignorant, feigning ignorance / pretending dumber than was, forced people to use common sense. Enabled continually exposing weaknesses in thinking, even in middle of city square → could end publicly made fool of.
- **Gadfly:** increasingly exasperating, especially to status-holders. Reputed saying: "Athens is like a sluggish horse, and I am the gadfly trying to sting it into life." (Gaarder's aside: What do we do with gadflies?)
- **Not to torment:** stinging not for torment; something within left no choice.

## 5. Divine Voice, Conscience, Trial and Death
- **Divine inner voice / conscience:** always said had "divine voice" inside him. E.g., protested against having part in condemning people to death; refused to inform on political enemies. Eventually cost life.
- **Accusation 399 BC:** "introducing new gods and corrupting the youth," plus not believing in accepted gods.
- **Verdict:** slender majority, jury of 500 found guilty.
- **Refusal to compromise:** could likely have appealed leniency, at least saved life by agreeing to leave Athens. Had he done so would not have been Socrates. Valued conscience — and truth — higher than life. Assured jury only acted in best interests of state. Condemned to drink **hemlock**; drank poison in presence of friends and died.
- **Why die?** question asked 2,400 years. Not only one to suffer death for convictions.

## 6. Socrates–Jesus Parallels (explicit in book)
- Both enigmatic even to contemporaries.
- Neither wrote teachings; rely on disciples' picture.
- Both masters of discourse, with characteristic self-assuredness that could fascinate and exasperate.
- Both believed spoke on behalf of something greater than themselves; challenged community power by criticizing injustice/corruption.
- Both cost lives; trials exhibit parallels: both could have saved themselves by mercy appeal, but felt mission would be betrayed unless kept faith to bitter end; by brave death commanded enormous following after death.
- Caveat: not alike; point is message inseparably linked to personal courage.

## 7. Philosopher vs Sophist: The Joker
- **Lived same time as Sophists**, same concern man/society not nature, but one significant difference: did not consider himself "sophist" = learned/wise person; did **not teach for money**. Called himself **philosopher** in true sense = "one who loves wisdom."
- **Central distinction for whole course:**
  - Sophists: take money for more or less hairsplitting expoundings; schoolmasters and self-opinionated know-it-alls satisfied with little they know, or boast knowing lot about subjects haven't faintest notion of.
  - Real philosopher = direct opposite: knows in reality knows very little; constantly strives for true insight. Socrates rare person: knew knew nothing about life/world, and **it troubled him**.
- **Formulation:** philosopher = someone who recognizes lot he does not understand, and is troubled by it; in that sense wiser than braggarts. "Wisest is she who knows she does not know." Socrates: **"One thing only I know, and that is that I know nothing."**
  - Rare admission even among philosophers; so dangerous in public can cost life. Most subversive people are those who ask questions. Giving answers not nearly as threatening. Any one question can be more explosive than thousand answers.
- **Emperor's new clothes / child:** emperor stark naked but none dared say; child burst "But he's got nothing on!" courageous like Socrates daring tell how little humans know. Similarity children–philosophers.
- **Two piles + joker:** mankind faced difficult questions with no satisfactory answers; two possibilities: fool self/world pretending know all, or shut eyes abandon progress. Humanity divided: **dead certain or totally indifferent** (both crawling deep in rabbit's fur). Like dividing deck into black/red piles, but from time to time **joker** turns up neither heart/club nor diamond/spade. Socrates joker in Athens: neither certain nor indifferent. All knew was knew nothing — and it troubled him. So became philosopher — one who does not give up but tirelessly pursues truth.
- **Oracle at Delphi story:** Athenian asked who wisest in Athens; oracle answered Socrates of all mortals wisest. Astounded/laughed, went straight to person city thought excessively wise; when person unable to give satisfactory answers, realized oracle right.
- **Foundation:** felt necessary to establish solid foundation for knowledge; foundation lay in **man's reason**. Unshakable faith in human reason → decidedly a **rationalist**.

## 8. Ethics: Right Insight → Right Action
- **Maxim:** guided by divine inner voice/conscience telling what was right. "He who knows what good is will do good."
- **Meaning:** right insight leads to right action. Only he who does right can be "virtuous man." When do wrong because don't know better. Hence important to go on learning.
- **Project:** finding clear and **universally valid definitions** of right/wrong. Unlike Sophists, ability to distinguish right/wrong lies in **people's reason, not society**.
- **Happiness argument:** thought no one could possibly be happy if acted against better judgment. He who knows how to achieve happiness will do so. Therefore he who knows what is right will do right — why would anybody choose to be unhappy?
  - Test questions: can live happy life if continually do things know deep down wrong? Lot lie/cheat/speak ill — aware not right/fair? Are they happy? Socrates: no.
- **Reason vs feeling (implicit):** reason eternal/immutable, basis for norms; not societal relativism or feeling.

## 9. Key Terms
- Sophist, skepticism, agnostic, rhetoric, Socratic irony, maieutics/midwife, rationalist, conscience/divine voice, virtue, universal/absolute norms vs relativism, gadfly, joker.

## 10. Legacy
- Founder-effect for many schools; model of lived philosophy where life/death inseparable from message; sets up Plato (pupil follows trial) and whole Athenian classical triad; establishes reason-based universal ethics vs relativism; method becomes Socratic/Platonic dialogue form.
