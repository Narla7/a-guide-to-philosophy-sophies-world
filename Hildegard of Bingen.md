# Hildegard of Bingen

*Context: Hildegard of Bingen (1098–1179) — Rhine Valley nun, preacher, physician and naturalist, witness in Gaarder to women's practical science and to Sophia, the female side of Wisdom.*

## 1. Life and Work — What the Book Gives

**Thesis:** Even in a male-dominated medieval church, women thinkers existed and were often more practical, even more scientific.

**Arguments / Details:**
- Hildegard of Bingen (1098–1179), nun in Rhine Valley.
- Roles: preacher, author, physician, botanist, naturalist.
- Proof women were often more practical, even more scientific, in the Middle Ages.
- Context: medieval church male-dominated; Aquinas's reported view makes woman as nature-being inferior (see [[Aquinas]]); Hildegard stands as counter-voice.

**Terms:** practical / scientific women in the Middle Ages.

**Legacy:** example of female authorship, medicine, and natural knowledge before universities.

## 2. Sophia — Wisdom and the Female Side of God

**Thesis:** Sophia (Greek for wisdom) names the ancient Jewish / Christian belief that God is not only male but has female side / "mother nature"; women also in God's likeness.

**Arguments / Details:**
- Sophia: Greek for wisdom; ancient Jewish / Christian belief that God is not only male but has female side / "mother nature"; women also in God's likeness.
- Significant for Jews and Greek Orthodox through Middle Ages; forgotten in West.
- Hildegard's vision: Sophia appears dressed in golden tunic with jewels.
- Name-play noted: Sophia / Sophie = wisdom — Sophie's own name ties her to this Wisdom tradition.

**Examples:** golden tunic with jewels vision; mother-nature imagery.

**Terms:** Sophia (Wisdom); mother nature; likeness of God.

## 3. Terms, Quotes, Legacy

**Terms:** Sophia; female side of God; practical science.
**Quotes:** Vision description — Sophia in golden tunic with jewels.
**Legacy:** preserves Wisdom theology; anticipates later recovery of feminine divine imagery; links philosophy's name (philo-sophia) to Sophie's story.
