# Augustine

*Context: St. Augustine (354–430) — the Church Father who christianized Plato for the Early Middle Ages, joining Greek philosophy to Biblical faith on illumination, evil, grace, and history.*

## 1. Life and Transition — Late Antiquity to Middle Ages

**Thesis:** Augustine embodies the transition from late antiquity to the Early Middle Ages, carrying Greek philosophy into Christianity.

**Arguments / Details:**
- Born Tagaste, North Africa; studies Carthage at 16; travels Rome, Milan; last years as bishop in Hippo near Carthage.
- Not lifelong Christian; tests religions / philosophies:
  - **Manichaeism:** half religion / half philosophy; dualism good/evil, light/darkness, spirit/matter; spirit rises above matter toward salvation.
  - **Stoicism:** attracts him because it denies sharp good/evil division.
  - **Neoplatonism:** decisive leaning — all existence divine in nature.
- Becomes Christian, but Christianity remains Platonically / Neoplatonically stamped. No dramatic break with Greece at the Middle Ages' entry; Church Fathers carry Greek philosophy over.
- Thought Plato must have known the Old Testament given Plato–Christian similarity (Gaarder calls this highly improbable); better formulation: Augustine "christianized" Plato, i.e. interpreted him so as not to threaten dogma.

**Examples:** Manichaean spirit-over-matter salvation; Stoic denial of sharp good/evil; Neoplatonic divine existence.

**Terms:** Church Fathers; christianizing Plato.

**Legacy:** Transmits Plato / Neoplatonism to Middle Ages; sets faith–reason problem; makes Greek–Christian continuity possible.

## 2. Faith, Reason, Illumination

**Thesis:** Reason has limits in religious questions; Christianity is divine mystery perceivable only through faith, yet faith gives a higher knowledge.

**Arguments:**
- Philosophy goes part of the way; peace requires faith.
- Belief lets God "illuminate" the soul to a supernatural knowledge of God.
- Only as Christian does he find inner peace.

**Quotes:**
- "Our heart is not quiet until it rests in Thee."

**Terms:** illumination.

## 3. Metaphysics — Creation Ex Nihilo and Ideas in God's Mind

**Thesis:** Preserve Platonic eternal Ideas within Biblical creationism by relocating them into the Divine mind.

**Arguments:**
- Against Greek eternity of world: God created world out of void (*ex nihilo*), a Biblical idea.
- Before creation, the "ideas" exist in the Divine mind. Thus eternal Ideas kept within Christian creationism.
- Example of bending Greek and Jewish thought together; Augustine is of two cultures.

**Terms:** *ex nihilo*; Ideas in God's mind.

## 4. Evil as Privation

**Thesis:** Evil has no independent existence; it is absence of good / absence of God.

**Arguments:**
- Youthful "problem of evil" (where does evil come from?) unsolved by Manichaean dualism.
- Neoplatonic / Plotinian solution adopted: evil is "absence of God"; God's creation is only good.
- Evil comes from human disobedience.

**Quotes:**
- "The good will is God's work; the evil will is the falling away from God's work."

**Terms:** privation theory (*privatio boni*) vs. dualism.

## 5. Anthropology — Soul, Body, Fall, Grace, Predestination / Foreknowledge

**Thesis:** Man is dual (mortal body + God-knowing soul) fallen and wholly dependent on grace, with salvation preordained yet responsibility retained.

**Arguments:**
- Rejects Plotinian "all is one": insurmountable barrier between God and world (Biblical ground).
- Yet man is spiritual: material body belonging to physical world ("moth and rust doth corrupt") plus soul that can know God. So "yes and no" to a divine soul.
- After the Fall, entire human race is lost; God decides certain people shall be saved from perdition.
- No one deserves redemption; who is saved / damned is preordained; wholly at God's mercy.
- Proof-text: Paul, Romans — potter and clay: "Hath not the potter power over the clay, of the same lump to make one vessel unto honour and another unto dishonour?"
- Sophie's objection (God playing / throwing away creatures; unfair; Socratic equal chances vs. two groups) answered in Gaarder's exposition as: Augustine expounds Biblical salvation / damnation, not an arbitrary division; he does not renounce responsibility / free will — we must live as among the chosen; God has "foreseen" how we will live. Tension with fate noted but left as foreknowledge + free will.
- Contrast stressed: distant from Athenian humanism.

**Terms:** Fall; grace; predestination / foreknowledge; free will vs. fate.

## 6. City of God and Philosophy of History

**Thesis:** All human history is struggle between "Kingdom of God" and "Kingdom of the World," played out in linear time toward God's Kingdom.

**Arguments:**
- "City / Kingdom of God" from Bible / Jesus.
- All human history is struggle between "Kingdom of God" and "Kingdom of the World" — not two political states but two powers struggling inside every person.
- Nevertheless Kingdom of God is more or less clearly present in the Church; Kingdom of the World in the State (e.g. declining Rome of Augustine's time).
- Later Church–State supremacy fight grows from this; "No salvation outside the Church"; Augustine's City becomes identified with established Church; protest only with 16th-century Reformation.
- First philosopher in the course to draw history into philosophy. Good/evil struggle is old; new is that it is played out in history.
- Against Plato here; follows Old Testament linear history: God needs all of history to realize Kingdom, enlighten man, destroy evil.

**Quotes:**
- "Divine foresight directs the history of mankind from Adam to the end of time as if it were the story of one man who gradually develops from childhood to old age."

**Terms:** City of God vs. City of the World; linear vs. cyclical history.

## 7. Terms, Legacy, Quotes Summary

**Terms:** illumination, privation of good (*privatio boni*), Fall, grace, predestination / foreknowledge, City of God vs. City of the World, linear vs. cyclical history.
**Legacy:** transmits Plato / Neoplatonism to Middle Ages; sets faith–reason problem; founds Christian philosophy of history; legitimates Church's salvific monopoly.
**Quotes:** "Our heart is not quiet until it rests in Thee." / "The good will is God's work; the evil will is the falling away from God's work." / "Hath not the potter power over the clay..." (Paul, Romans) / "Divine foresight directs the history of mankind from Adam to the end of time as if it were the story of one man who gradually develops from childhood to old age."
