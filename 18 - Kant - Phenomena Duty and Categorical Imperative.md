# 18 - Kant - Phenomena, Duty and Categorical Imperative

Context: Gaarder presents Kant as the synthesis that ends the rationalism vs empiricism struggle and grounds modern epistemology, limits of reason, and duty ethics.

## 1. Life and Project

- **Immanuel Kant (1724-1804), Königsberg, East Prussia**, son of a master saddler, deeply pious family background. Lived practically all his life in Königsberg until age eighty.
- Like Berkeley, wanted to preserve foundations of Christian belief, but by critique, not dogma.
- **First professor of philosophy** in Sophie's World so far. Both kinds of philosopher: expert on history of philosophy AND original system-builder.
- Solid grounding in tradition: rationalism (Descartes, Spinoza) that basis of knowledge lies in mind + empiricism (Locke, Berkeley, Hume) that knowledge proceeds from senses, with Hume's limit: we cannot prove necessary connection from sense perception.
- Kant's verdict: both partly right, partly wrong. Central question since Descartes: **what can we know about the world?** Two crude options: world is exactly as we perceive it, or as reason presents it. Kant: both sensing and reason contribute.

> Kant thought that both 'sensing' and 'reason' come into play in our conception of the world.

## 2. Copernican Revolution: Mind Shapes World

- Agrees with Hume/empiricists: all knowledge of world comes from sensations.
- Extends hand to rationalists: in reason are decisive factors that determine how we perceive world. Certain conditions in human mind contribute to conception of world.

### Red glasses experiment
- Sophie puts on red-lensed glasses: pale becomes pink, dark becomes crimson, but same world.
- Glasses limit way you perceive reality. Everything you see is part of world, but how you see determined by glasses. Cannot say world is red though you conceive it so.
- Walk in woods or home to Captain's Bend: whatever you saw would be red as long as glasses on.
- Point: **there are certain conditions governing mind's operation which influence way we experience world. We cannot take off glasses of reason.**

### Time and space as forms of intuition
- Whatever we see will first and foremost be perceived as phenomena **in time and space**.
- Kant calls time and space our two **forms of intuition** [Anschauungsformen]. They precede every experience. Before experience we know we will perceive as in time and space.
- Whether raised India or Greenland content differs, but form universal: world as series of processes in time and space. Can be said beforehand.
- **Time and space belong to human condition. They are modes of perception, not attributes of physical world.** Radical new way.
- Mind not passive wax receiving sensations. Mind leaves imprint.
- Analogy: water poured into glass pitcher adapts to pitcher's form. Perceptions adapt to forms of intuition.

> Kant claimed that it is not only mind which conforms to things. Things also conform to the mind. Kant called this the Copernican Revolution in the problem of human knowledge.

- As Copernicus said earth revolves around sun not vice versa, Kant reverses: objects conform to our cognition.

### Causality as mental attribute
- Hume: only habit makes us see causal link; cannot perceive black billiard ball as cause of white ball movement, cannot prove always will.
- Kant turns it: what Hume says cannot be proved is attribute of human reason. Law of causality eternal and absolute **because human reason perceives everything as cause and effect.**
- Common intuition says causality lies in physical world; Kant says inherent in us.

### Cat vs human ball example
- Cat lying living room, ball rolls in: cat runs after ball.
- Human same room: first turns around to see where ball came from.
- Because human inevitably looks for cause of every event; law of causality part of makeup.
- Child may not yet turn: child's reason not fully developed until had sensory material to work with. Senseless to talk about empty mind.

### Summary formula: matter + form
- Two elements of knowledge:
  1. **Material of knowledge**: external conditions cannot know before perceived through senses.
  2. **Form of knowledge**: internal conditions - perception as in time/space, conforming to unbreakable causality.
- Before go out morning cannot know what will see, but know will be in time/space and causality will apply because carry it as consciousness.
- Could have been made differently: different sensory apparatus, different time/space sense, might not search for cause.

## 3. Phenomena vs Noumena: Das Ding an sich

- Greatest contribution: dividing line between **things in themselves - das Ding an sich - and things as they appear to us / thing for me**.
- Never certain knowledge of things in themselves. Only know how things appear to us.
- But prior to experience can say something about how human mind will perceive.
- Agrees with Hume cannot know with certainty what world is like in itself, only for me / for everybody.

## 4. Limits of Reason: Antinomies and Unanswerable Questions

- Mind's glasses set clear limits.
- Old big questions: immortal soul? God? nature consists tiny indivisible particles? universe finite or infinite? world had beginning?
- Kant does not brush aside; philosopher must face them.
- Reason operates beyond limits of comprehension in these questions, yet basic desire in our nature to pose them.
- Asking about totality of which we ourselves tiny part: never completely know totality.

### Reason on hold
- Normal cognition = sensory material + rational form (e.g., seek cause like rolling ball).
- In cosmological questions reason is on hold: no sensory material to process, no experience of whole great reality we are tiny part of.
- Analogy: we are tiny part of ball rolling across floor, cannot know where came from, yet attribute of reason always to ask where ball comes from. We ask and ask, exert to fullest, never get firm bite, never satisfactory answer because reason not locked on.

### Two equally reasonable/unreasonable viewpoints
- In nature of reality always two contrasting viewpoints equally likely/unlikely as far as reason tells:

1. **Finite vs infinite universe:** just as meaningful world must have beginning in time as had no beginning. If always existed, how can anything always existed if never beginning? Forced to opposite: must have begun sometime from nothing unless change from one state to another. But can something come from nothing? Both problematic, yet one must be right.
2. **Divisibility:** Democritus/materialists: nature must consist minimal parts everything made of. Descartes: extended reality always divisible into ever smaller parts. Which right? Both. Neither.
3. **Freedom vs necessity:** many named freedom most important value, yet Stoics, Spinoza: everything happens through necessity of natural law. Reason unable certain judgment.
4. **God:** rationalists like Descartes tried prove must be God because have idea supreme being. Aristotle/Aquinas: must be God because everything must have first cause. Kant rejects both proofs. Neither reason nor experience certain basis. As far as reason, just as likely as unlikely God exists.

### Faith and practical postulates
- Where reason and experience fall short, vacuum can be filled by faith. How he saved Christianity.
- Note: Kant Protestant. Since Reformation Protestantism emphasizes faith; Catholic Church since early Middle Ages believed more in reason as pillar of faith.
- Further: essential for morality to **presuppose immortal soul, God's existence, free will**.
- Unlike Descartes who first critical then smuggles God by reason, Kant emphasizes brought by faith, not reason. Calls them **practical postulates**: postulate = assume what cannot be proved; practical = for sake of praxis/practice, man's morality.
- Quote paraphrase: It is a moral necessity to assume existence of God.

### Major's note paradox
- Hilde's father inserts via Little Red Ridinghood: If human brain simple enough to understand, still so stupid couldn't understand it. Alberto glosses: cannot expect to understand what we are. Maybe comprehend flower/insect, never ourselves, even less universe. Kantian limit.

## 5. Ethics in Full Detail

- Hume's skepticism forces rethink. Hume: never prove right/wrong, cannot derive ought from is; right/wrong determined sentiments. Too tenuous for Kant.
- Kant with rationalists: difference right/wrong matter of reason, not sentiment. Ability distinguish inherent, not learned but born in mind. Everybody has **practical reason** - intelligence to discern right/wrong in every case.
- Innate like other attributes: just as perceive causal relation, all have access same **universal moral law**.
- Moral law same absolute validity as physical laws. As basic to morality as everything has cause, or 7+5=12, basic to intelligence.

### Formal and universal
- Since precedes every experience, **formal**: not bound to particular situation. Applies all people all societies all times. Does not say do this if in that situation. Says how behave in all situations.

### Categorical imperative - formulations
- Moral law as **categorical imperative**: categorical = applies all situations; imperative = commanding, absolutely authoritative.

1. **Universal Law formulation:** *Act as if the maxim of your action were to become through your will a Universal Law of Nature.*
   - Gloss Sophie: when do something, make sure want everybody else do same if same situation. Only then acting according to moral law within.
   - Example implied: lying/promise - if maxim lying to get advantage universalized, promises meaningless, contradiction. Gaarder does not spell lying example at length here but stresses test: can you will everyone do same?
2. **Humanity as end formulation:** *Act in such a way that you always treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never simply as a means, but always at the same time as an end.*
   - Must not exploit others to own advantage. Every man end in himself. Applies also to self: must not exploit self as mere means.
- Reminds golden rule: Do unto others... Also formal rule covering all ethical choices. Basically same as Kant's universal law.

### Unprovable like causality and conscience
- Hume objection: can't prove right/wrong by reason.
- Kant reply: moral law just as absolute/universal as causality. Cannot be proved either, nevertheless absolute unalterable. Nobody denies.
- When describes law of morals, describing human conscience. Cannot prove what conscience tells, but know nevertheless.

### Duty ethics and good will
- Example: kind/helpful only because pays off, to become popular: acting **in accordance** with moral law, fair enough, but not **moral action** unless conquered self.
- Only when do something **purely out of duty** can be called moral. Hence **duty ethics**.
- Example: feel duty collect money for Red Cross or church bazaar. Important do because know right. Even if money lost in street, or insufficient to feed mouths, obeyed moral law. Acted out of **good will**, and good will determines whether morally right, not consequences. Hence **good will ethic**.
- Why important to know exactly when acts out of respect? Surely most important what really helps others? Kant would not disagree help matters, but only when know acting out of respect for moral law are we acting freely.

### Freedom, dual nature, autonomy
- Peculiar: act freely only when obey law?
- Need postulate free will, yet everything obeys causality. How free?
- Divides man like Descartes dual body/mind:
  - As **material creatures**, wholly mercy of causality's unbreakable law. Do not decide what perceive - perception comes through necessity influences whether like or not. No free will.
  - As **creatures of reason**, part in das Ding an sich - world as exists itself independent sensory impressions. Only when follow practical reason enabling moral choices exercise free will, because when conform to moral law, **we who make law we conform to**.
- Sophie intuits: me or something in me tells not be mean.
- When choose not be mean even against own interests, acting freely.
- Not free if just do whatever want. Can become slave to things, even egoism. Independence/freedom required to rise above desires/vices.
- Animals: just follow inclinations/needs, no freedom to follow moral law. Difference animals/humans.

### Hypothetical vs categorical (implied)
- Gaarder does not use term hypothetical imperative here, but contrasts acting to be popular / pays off (conditional, instrumental) vs acting because right (unconditional, categorical). Former in accordance, latter out of duty.

## 6. Aesthetics, Teleology, Politics (as mentioned)

- **Aesthetics:** In Romanticism chapter Gaarder recalls: Kant in aesthetics investigated what happens when overwhelmed by beauty - work of art. When abandon ourselves with no other intention than aesthetic experience itself, brought closer to experience of das Ding an sich. Artist plays freely on faculty of cognition. Schiller develops: activity of artist like playing, man only free when plays, because makes own rules.
- **Perpetual Peace (1795):** Kant advocated league of nations. All countries unite to assure peaceful coexistence, emerge from wild state of nature which creates wars, contract to keep peace. Laborious road, duty to work for universal and lasting securing of peace. Far-distant goal, almost philosophy's ultimate goal. ~125 years later League of Nations after WWI, replaced by UN after WWII. Kant father of UN idea. Man's practical reason requires it.

## 7. Closing Saying

- With Kant era in history philosophy at an end. Died 1804 when Romanticism ascendant.
- Gravestone Königsberg: ***Two things fill my mind with ever-increasing wonder and awe, the more often and the more intensely the reflection dwells on them: the starry heavens above me and the moral law within me.***
- Opening epigraph same line.
- Alberto's application (story, minimize): seven plus five is twelve survives all comic-strip effects; philosophy opposite fairy tales - i.e., rational necessity free from manipulated perception.

### Quotes to remember
- Things also conform to mind.
- Time and space are first and foremost modes of perception and not attributes of the physical world.
- It is a moral necessity to assume the existence of God.
- Act as if the maxim of your action were to become through your will a Universal Law of Nature.
- Act so you treat humanity never simply as a means, but always at the same time as an end.
- Starry heavens above me and moral law within me.
