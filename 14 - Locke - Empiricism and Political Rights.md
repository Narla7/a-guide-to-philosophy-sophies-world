# 14 – Locke: Empiricism and Political Rights

*Context: John Locke (1632–1704), first great British empiricist, answers Continental rationalism (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz) – no innate ideas; all knowledge from experience; plus liberal politics of tolerance, equality, and divided power.*

## 1. Life and Project

- **John Locke 1632–1704 (same birth year as Spinoza), Englishman.** Main work ***Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1690).**
- Two questions: (1) Where do we get ideas from? (2) Can we rely on what senses tell us / is world as we perceive it?
- Contrast: **Rationalists** (Descartes French, Spinoza Dutch, Leibniz German – Continental rationalism): reason primary source; innate ideas prior to experience; clearer idea → more certain corresponds to reality (e.g. Descartes's perfect entity → God exists).
- **Empiricists** (Locke, Berkeley, Hume – British empiricism): derive all world-knowledge from senses. Classic formula from Aristotle, repeated by Locke against Descartes: **"There is nothing in the mind except what was first in the senses."** I.e. no innate ideas; conception unrelated to experienced facts (God, eternity, substance) is false/misuse of reason; learned systems may be impressive fantasy. Must pan for gold: scrutinize conceptions under microscope, purify hollow notions, keep only what traces to experience. Like tidying thoughts and libraries.

## 2. Tabula Rasa – Sensation and Reflection

- Before perception mind is ***tabula rasa* – empty slate; bare as blackboard before teacher; unfurnished room.**
- Then sensing begins – most intensely in infants: see, smell, taste, feel, hear → **simple ideas of sense.**
- Mind not merely passive receiver: activity of thinking, reasoning, believing, doubting works on sense ideas → **reflection.** Distinction **sensation vs. reflection;** mind classifies/processes streaming sensations – hence must be on guard.
- Only **simple sensations** perceived directly. Apple example: we don't sense whole "apple" at once; receive series – green, fresh smell, juicy/sharp taste. After many apples form **complex idea** of "apple." Infant tasting first time has no "apple" concept, only green/fresh/juicy/sour; bundles similar sensations into concepts apple/pear/orange. All material of world-knowledge comes through sensations; untraceable = false, must be rejected.
- Angel/crocophant preview (developed by Hume): complex ideas pasted together without corresponding object must be rejected.

## 3. Primary vs. Secondary Qualities

- Second question: is world as perceived? Don't jump to conclusions.
- **Primary qualities:** extension, weight, motion, number, size, density, gravity – lie within objects themselves; senses reproduce objectively; all can agree. Cannot "think" round orange square; cannot "think" 200g weighs 8kg (may believe, but wrong); peas in can either 986 or not; car either moving or stationary; guessers one more right.
- **Secondary qualities:** color, smell, taste, sound, hot/cold, sweet/sour – reproduce only effect of outer reality on our senses; vary person/animal/sense-organ; neither right/wrong, pretty/ugly. Joanna finds orange sour (face like lemon, one segment), Sophie sweet – both describe affection, not orange-in-itself.
- On extended reality Locke agrees with Descartes: it has qualities reason can grasp.
- Admits **intuitive/demonstrative knowledge** elsewhere too – rationalistic residue.

## 4. Rationalistic Residues – God, Ethics, Tolerance

- Holds certain ethical principles apply to everyone – **natural right;** inherent in reason to know **God exists** – idea born of reason, not faith alone.
- Speaks for intellectual liberty, tolerance.
- Sexual equality: subjugation of women to men "man-made," alterable; forerunner of liberal ideas flowering in 18th-century French Enlightenment; influence on John Stuart Mill, key in equality struggle.
- Gaarder notes Locke's "natural right" reappears in Hume chapter (rationalists from Socrates to Locke assume right/wrong inherent in reason – Hume will deny, grounding morals in sentiment).

## 5. Politics – Division of Powers (Seed in Text)

- Text's explicit politics is brief but load-bearing:
  - First to advocate **division of powers** principle, later fully flowered in Enlightenment, formulated by Montesquieu: legislative (elected representatives make laws), judicial (courts), executive (government/king applies laws).
  - Locke's emphasis: **legislative and executive must be separated to avoid tyranny.** Lived under Louis XIV – assembled all power: "I am the State" (*L'État, c'est moi*), absolute, lawless/arbitrary rule. Legal State requires people's representatives make laws, king/government apply them.
- Standard Lockean theory behind Gaarder's сжатый summary (to complete requested headings – background, lightly beyond letter of chapter):
  - **Natural rights / state of nature:** life, liberty, property; reason teaches natural law.
  - **Social contract:** government by consent to protect rights; trust, not absolute submission (vs. Hobbes).
  - **Property:** mixing labour with nature makes property; limits, money.
  - **Right of rebellion/resistance:** tyrannical breach of trust dissolves government, power returns to people.
  - Gaarder covers these themes at Enlightenment level (natural rights of citizen) rather than in Locke chapter itself; Locke chapter gives only natural right + legislative/executive separation as seed.

> Quotes: "There is nothing in the mind except what was first in the senses." / Mind as "tabula rasa – or an empty slate" / "as bare and empty as a blackboard before the teacher arrives" / "unfurnished room." / Primary: "extension, weight, motion and number"; secondary: colour, smell, taste, sound – "Everyone to his own taste."
