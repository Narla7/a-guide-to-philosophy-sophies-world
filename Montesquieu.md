# Montesquieu

*Context: French Enlightenment (mid-18th century). After Hume, Alberto gives Sophie the French Enlightenment as seven-point program. Philosophical center of gravity: **England first half, France middle, Germany toward end**. Important French names: **Montesquieu, Voltaire, and Rousseau**. Dating anchors: Hume died 1776, about 20 years after Montesquieu, only 2 years before Voltaire and Rousseau (both 1778). All three had been to England, knew Locke.*

## 1. Division of powers: Locke to Montesquieu

- Previous lesson tail (Locke forerunner of liberal Enlightenment ideas) states Locke first advocated **division of powers**.
  - Question: isn't that when state power divided between institutions?
  - Answer in text: legislative power = elected representatives; judicial = law courts; executive = government.
- **Montesquieu** origin: division originates from French Enlightenment philosopher Montesquieu. Locke emphasized legislative and executive must be separated to avoid tyranny; lived under Louis XIV who assembled all power ("I am the State"), absolute ruler — now would call lawless/arbitrary. Locke view: to ensure legal State, people's representatives must make laws, king/government must apply them.
- No further separate Montesquieu biography in source chunk; his contribution carried by this doctrine.

## 2. Shared Enlightenment program he belongs to

1. Opposition to authority
2. Rationalism
3. The enlightenment movement
4. Cultural optimism
5. The return to nature
6. Natural religion
7. Human rights

- Many French philosophers visited England, more liberal than home, intrigued by English natural science, especially **Newton and universal physics**, and British philosophy, especially Locke's political philosophy.
- Back in France, increasingly opposed old authority; essential to remain skeptical of inherited truths; individual must find own answer to every question. Descartes tradition inspiring.
- Directed not least against power of **clergy, king, nobility** — far more powerful in 18th-century France than England.
- Revolutionary ideas arose much earlier than **French Revolution 1789**.
- Slogan later hung in cabin: **LIBERTY, EQUALITY AND FRATERNITY**.

## Quotes to keep

- "I am the State" (Louis XIV as negative example); seven-point list; Liberty Equality Fraternity.
