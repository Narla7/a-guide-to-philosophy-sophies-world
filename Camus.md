## 1. What the book actually covers
- French **Albert Camus** listed among writers influenced by existentialism 1940s–present, especially drama, alongside Sartre (plays/novels), Irish **Samuel Beckett**, Romanian **Eugène Ionesco**, Polish **Witold Gombrowicz**.
- Characteristic style = **absurdism**, esp. "theater of the absurd."
- Absurd = meaningless/irrational.
- Aim paradox: show lack of meaning to get audience to **disagree**; not cultivate meaningless; by exposing absurd in ordinary situations force onlookers to seek truer, more essential life.
- Devices:
  - Absolutely trivial situations → **hyperrealism**: reproduce exactly bathroom morning in ordinary home on stage → audience laughs; laughter interpretable as **defense mechanism** against seeing self lampooned.
  - Surrealistic/dreamlike situations accepted by characters **without surprise** → audience compelled to react in surprise at lack of surprise. Method compared to Chaplin silents: laconic acceptance of absurd compels audience to look inward for genuine/true.
- Existential maxim: at times right to feel must get away even without knowing where to go — if house catches fire must get out even with nowhere to live.
