# 06 - Plato - Forms Cave Soul and State

*Chapter context: Video-Plato's four tasks (cookies, horses, immortal soul, men/women) introduce Plato (428-347 BC), Socrates' pupil, founder of the Academy.*

## 1. Life, Academy, Works — Thesis
- **Dates:** 428-347 BC; 29 when Socrates drank hemlock; pupil for some time, followed trial closely.
- **Formative trauma:** Athens condemning noblest citizen to death more than impressed him — shaped entire philosophic endeavor; conflict between society as really is vs true/ideal society.
- **First deed:** published **Socrates' Apology** — account of plea to large jury.
- **Preservation contrast:** Socrates wrote nothing; many pre-Socratics wrote but hardly any remains. Plato: believed all principal works preserved — Apology, collection of Epistles, ~25 philosophical Dialogues.
- **Academy:** own school in grove not far from Athens named after legendary hero **Academus** → Academy. Origin of thousands of "academies," "academics," "academic subjects."
  - Subjects: philosophy, mathematics, gymnastics — though "taught" hardly right word; **lively discourse** most important; not by chance writings took form of dialogues.

## 2. Philosophical Project: Eternal vs Flowing; True/Beautiful/Good
- **Question:** what was Plato concerned with? Relationship between **eternal/immutable** vs what **"flows."** Same as pre-Socratics in form.
- **Sophists/Socrates twist:** turned to morals/society ideals/virtues. Sophists: right/wrong varies city to city, generation to generation → flows. Socrates unacceptable: eternal absolute rules for right/wrong, reachable by common sense because human reason eternal/immutable.
- **Plato's synthesis:** concerned with BOTH eternal/immutable in nature AND in morals/society; to him one and same. Tried to grasp "reality" eternal/immutable.
- **Role of philosophers:** not to choose beauty queen or day's bargain in tomatoes (why often unpopular); to ignore topical affairs, draw attention to eternally "true," eternally "beautiful," eternally "good."

## 3. Theory of Ideas/Forms — Core
- **Agreement with Empedocles/Democritus:** in natural world everything flows, must be something never changes (four roots/atoms). Agreed as proposition but in quite different way.
- **No material substance:** everything tangible flows; no substances that do not dissolve; everything material time can erode, but everything made after timeless **mold/form** eternal/immutable.
- **Not physical basic substance:** not atoms/roots; eternal immutable patterns, spiritual/abstract, all things fashioned after.
- **Critique of atomists:** pre-Socratics explained change without presupposing change (eternal smallest elements not dissolving) — fair enough. But no reasonable explanation how smallest elements once building blocks in horse could whirl together 400-500 years later fashion completely new horse, elephant, crocodile. Atoms never fashion themselves into "eledile" or "crocophant." That set reflections going.
- **Examples:**
  - **Horses:** particular horse flows — old/lame, dies. "Form" of horse eternal/immutable. Why all horses same? Not that identical (brown as bears vs white as lambs, sizes), but share something enabling identification as horses; never horse with six/eight legs, never cross horse-pig. Behind every horse/pig/human: "idea horse," "idea pig," "idea human being."
  - **Lego horse:** box of Lego, build horse, take apart, put blocks back; cannot expect new horse by shaking box. Blocks won't find each other. Must rebuild because picture in mind unchanged from horse to horse; model remains.
  - **Fifty cookies / gingerbread men:** baker bakes fifty absolutely identical cookies? Mother's Christmas gingerbread men: not perfect (thicker, broken) but in way exactly same because **same mold**. Alien from outer space seeing 50 identical gingerbread men (one missing arm, one lost head bit, one bump) concludes common origin, desires to see mold; mold utter perfection, more beautiful than crude copies. Bakery has more than one mold (men, horses, pigs) but one mold enough per type. Gaarder: if solved alone, arrived exactly as Plato did — dropping from outer space, standing on tip of rabbit fur hair, astonished phenomena so alike, concluded limited forms behind all we see. Called forms **ideas**.
  - **Chicken/idea-chicken (bridge to Aristotle):** idea chicken existed in world of ideas long before sensory chickens.
- **Terminology:** reality behind material world = **world of ideas** containing eternal immutable patterns behind phenomena. Known as **Plato's theory of ideas**.

## 4. True Knowledge (episteme) vs Opinion (doxa)
- **Seriousness:** probably didn't believe literally same way all life, but in some dialogues certainly means to be understood so.
- **Philosopher's need:** tries to grasp eternal/immutable; pointless to write treatise on particular soap bubble — bursts before study, no market for what nobody saw and existed five seconds.
- **Sensory world = soap bubble:** everything tangible likened to soap bubble, nothing lasting; human/animal dies/decomposes; even marble disintegrates (Acropolis falling into ruin — scandal but way it is).
- **Consequence:** never true knowledge of what constantly changes; only **opinions** about sensory/tangible things; **true knowledge** only of things understood with reason.
- **Mold inference:** lopsided gingerbread man hard to see what meant; having seen dozens more/less successful, sure what mold like, guess though never seen. Seeing actual mold with eyes not even advantage because senses vary person to person; reason same for everyone, reliable.
- **Classroom example:** teacher asks prettiest rainbow colour → many answers (thinking so/feeling). Asks 8×3 → whole class same (hopefully) because reason speaking; reason direct opposite of thinking so/feeling; eternal/universal because expresses eternal/universal states.
- **Mathematics absorbing:** mathematical states never change → true knowledge.
  - **Pinecone:** find round pinecone; you think completely round, Joanna insists flattened one side → argue! Cannot have true knowledge of what perceive with eyes.
  - **Ideal circle:** sum angles in circle 360 degrees with absolute certainty; talking about ideal circle may not exist physically but clearly visualized; dealing with hidden mold not kitchen-table cookie.
  - **Triangle:** angles sum 180 degrees to end of time.
  - **Idea horse:** walks on four legs even if all sensory horses break leg.
- **Summary:** inexact conceptions of sensed things; true knowledge of reasoned things.

## 5. Anthropology: Dual Creature, Immortal Soul, Recollection, Eros
- **Two regions of reality:**
  1. World of senses: only approximate/incomplete knowledge via five approximate/incomplete senses; everything flows, nothing permanent; nothing IS, only things come to be/pass away.
  2. World of ideas: true knowledge via reason; cannot be perceived by senses, but ideas/forms eternal/immutable.
- **Man dual:** body flows, bound to senses, same fate as all else (soap bubble); senses based in body, unreliable. Plus **immortal soul** — realm of reason; not physical, can survey world of ideas.
- **Pre-existence & forgetting:** soul existed before inhabiting body (lying on shelf with cookie molds). As soon wakes in human body, forgotten all perfect ideas.
- **Recollection trigger:** as human discovers forms in nature, vague recollection stirs. Sees horse — imperfect (gingerbread horse) — sufficient to awaken faint recollection of perfect "horse" once seen in world of ideas; stirs yearning to return to true realm. Calls yearning **eros = love**. Soul experiences "longing to return to true origin." Hence book chapter subtitle: longing to return to realm of soul.
- **Flight:** body/sensory world experienced as imperfect/insignificant; soul yearns to fly home on wings of love to world of ideas, freed from chains of body.
- **Ideal course / philosophers' way:** by no means all set soul free; most cling to reflections. See horse and another horse but never that of which every horse feeble imitation (rush kitchen stuff gingerbread without thought where came from). Philosophy = description of philosophic practice.
- **Shadow analogy:** see shadow, assume caster; see animal-shadow, think may be horse but unsure, turn see horse itself infinitely more beautiful/sharper than blurred horse-shadow. Similarly all natural phenomena merely shadows of eternal forms. Most content among shadows, think shadows all there are, never realize even are shadows, pay no heed to immortality of own soul.

## 6. Myth of the Cave — Full Detail
- **Location in work:** in dialogue **Republic**; retold in Gaarder's own words.
- **Setup:** people living underground cave, backs to mouth, hands/feet bound so only look at back wall. Behind them high wall, behind wall pass human-like creatures holding various figures above top. Behind figures fire → cast flickering shadows on back wall. Only thing dwellers see = shadow play. Sitting since birth → think shadows all there are.
- **Liberation:** one manages free bonds. First asks where shadows come from. Turns, sees figures above wall. Dazzled by sharp sunlight and clarity (only seen shadow). If climbs over wall past fire into outside, even more dazzled. After rubbing eyes struck by beauty: first time colours/clear shapes; real animals/flowers cave shadows poor reflections of. Asks where animals/flowers come from. Sees **sun** in sky, realizes gives life to flowers/animals just as fire made shadows visible.
- **Return:** joyful dweller could skip countryside delighting freedom, but thinks of others still down. Goes back. Tries convince shadows but flickering reflections of real things. Don't believe; point to wall say what see is all there is. Finally **kill him**.
- **Meaning:**
  - Philosopher's road from shadowy images to true ideas behind phenomena.
  - Probably also thinking of **Socrates**, whom cave dwellers killed because disturbed conventional ideas, tried light way to true insight. Illustrates Socrates' courage and pedagogic responsibility.
  - Relationship darkness of cave vs beyond = forms of natural world vs world of ideas. Not that natural world dark/dreary, but dark/dreary compared to clarity of ideas. Picture of beautiful landscape not dark either, but only picture.
- **Sophie's experience parallel (narrative pointer only):** sun rising as reads of climbing out, feeling emerged, seeing nature differently, colour-blind healed; questioning if all living imperfect copies; squirrel/form, golden nugget soul that cannot corrode.

## 7. The Philosophic State (Republic; Laws)
- **Utopian/ideal state:** imaginary ideal = Utopian state. Briefly: state should be governed by **philosophers**. Bases on construction of human body.
- **Tripartite man:**
  - Body: **head / chest / abdomen**.
  - Soul faculty: **reason / will / appetite**.
  - Virtue/ideal: **wisdom / courage / temperance** (appetite must be curbed so temperance exercised).
  - Harmony: only when three parts function together as unity get harmonious/virtuous individual. School: child first curb appetites, then develop courage, finally reason leads wisdom.
- **State built exactly like body** (Greek medical science model): rulers / auxiliaries / laborers (farmers etc.).
  - Healthy harmonious man exercises balance/temperance; virtuous state characterized by everyone knowing place in overall picture.
- **Rationalism:** creation of good state depends on governed with reason. Just as head governs body, philosophers must rule society.
- **Table:**
  | BODY | SOUL | VIRTUE | STATE |
  | head | reason | wisdom | rulers |
  | chest | will | courage | auxiliaries |
  | abdomen | appetite | temperance | laborers |
- **Caste parallel:** not unlike old Hindu caste system where each person particular function for whole; even before Plato tripartite auxiliary/priest, warrior, laborer. Nowadays perhaps call totalitarian.
- **Women rulers — progressive for time:**
  - Believed women could govern as effectively as men because rulers govern by reason; women exactly same reasoning powers as men, provided same training and exempt from child-rearing/housekeeping.
  - Rulers/warriors not allowed family life or private property. Child-rearing too important to leave individual, responsibility of state. First philosopher to advocate **state-organized nursery schools and full-time education**.
- **Later work Laws:** after significant political setbacks, described **constitutional state** as next-best. Reintroduced private property and family ties. Women's freedom more restricted. But said state that does not educate/train women like man who only trains right arm.
- **Overall:** positive view considering time. In **Symposium**, gives woman, legendary priestess **Diotima**, honor of having given Socrates philosophic insight. Eros/ladder of love tradition linked here: soul's yearning taught via Diotima.
- **Critic:** first critic pupil from own Academy: **Aristotle**, third great philosopher from Athens.
