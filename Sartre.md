# Sartre

*Split from [[25 - Existentialism and Our Time - Sartre Freedom and Meaning|25 - Existentialism and Our Time]] §2–§4 — full detail preserved. Overview kept in source file.*

*Context: French Jean-Paul Sartre (1905–1980), leading light to broader public, especially popular in 1940s just after war; later allied with Marxist movement in France, never party member. Met lifelong companion Simone de Beauvoir in a cafe.*

## 1. Existence precedes essence
- Slogan glossed: **"existentialism is humanism"** — starts from nothing but humanity itself; bleaker than Renaissance humanism.
- Both Kierkegaard and some modern existentialists Christian; **Sartre atheistic**: merciless analysis when **"God is dead"** (phrase from Nietzsche).
- Key word **existence** ≠ merely being alive. Plants/animals alive/exist but need not think what it implies. Man alone conscious of own existence.
- Material thing simply **"in itself"**; mankind **"for itself."** Being of man ≠ being of things.
- Formula: **"Existence takes priority over essence."**
  - Essence = what something consists of, nature/being.
  - Man has **no innate nature**; must create self, create own essence, because not fixed in advance.
  - Vain through whole history to seek what man/human nature is; useless to search for meaning of life in general.
  - **Condemned to improvise**: like actors dragged onto stage without learned lines, no script, no prompter whispering directions. Must decide how to live. Bible/philosophy book cannot supply practical answer.
- Consequence: when realize alive, will die, no meaning to cling to → **angst** (Kierkegaard's term retained).
- **Alienation** language from Hegel/Marx: feeling alien in meaningless world creates despair, boredom, nausea, absurdity. Described as 20th-c. city dweller's state; normal to feel depressed / everything too boring.

> "The fact that I exist takes priority over what I am."

## 2. Condemned to be free — responsibility
- Renaissance humanists triumphantly stressed freedom/independence; Sartre experiences freedom as **curse**:
> "Man is condemned to be free. Condemned because he has not created himself — and is nevertheless free. Because having once been hurled into the world, he is responsible for everything he does."
- We did not ask to be created free — precisely Sartre's point — yet are free, condemned to choose throughout life.
- No eternal values/norms to adhere to → choices even more significant; totally responsible for everything we do.
- Forbidden moves:
  - Disclaim responsibility because "must" go to work, "must" live up to middle-class expectations.
  - Slip into anonymous masses → only members of impersonal flock, fled from self into **self-deception**.
- Positive demand: freedom obliges to make something of self, live **"authentically" / "truly."**
- Ethics especially: never lay blame on "human nature," "human frailty." Grown men behaving like pigs blaming "old Adam": **there is no old Adam**, merely figure clutched to avoid responsibility.
- Sophie's objection — ought to be limit to blame — left standing; Sartre's rigor presented without softening.
- **Not nihilism**: nihilist = nothing means anything, everything permissible. Sartre: life **must** have meaning, imperative; **we ourselves must create it**. "To exist is to create your own life."

## 4. Consciousness, perception, the absent Pierre
- Sartre's proof-attempt: consciousness in itself is **nothing until it has perceived something**; consciousness always conscious **of** something; "something" provided as much by ourselves as surroundings; partly decide what we perceive by selecting what is significant.
- Examples:
  - Two people same room experience quite differently; contribute own meaning/interests.
  - Pregnant woman thinks she sees pregnant women everywhere — not because none before, but now sees through different eyes.
  - Escaped convict sees policemen everywhere.
  - What is of no interest, don't see.
- Staged demonstration: Alberto deliberately 15 min late ("academic quarter").
  - Sophie's first notice: **that Alberto was absent**. Strange to notice what is absent, but she was to meet him.
  - Sartre uses just such cafe visit to show we **"annihilate"** irrelevant.
  - Lovers' analogues: waiting for loved one's call, "hear" him **not calling** all evening; arrange station meeting, crowds milling, cannot see him — all in way, unimportant, aggravating, taking too much room; only register **he is not there**.
