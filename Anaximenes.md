# Anaximenes
*Pre-Socratic natural philosopher — from Sophie's World, extreme-detail summary. Chapter context: first systematic philosophers ask — is there a basic substance everything is made of, and how can it change? (Water into wine? Earth + water into a live frog?)*

The natural philosophers were the earliest Greek philosophers, each with his own "project" — what he was especially concerned to find out. They assumed "something" had always existed and asked what basic substance lies at the root of all change — how live fish come from water, trees from dead earth, a baby from a womb — seeking underlying laws by studying nature itself rather than myths. Only fragments survive, mostly via Aristotle two centuries later, who reports conclusions more than paths; what matters is the questions they asked and the type of answer they sought.

## Core thesis
The source of all things is **"air" / "vapor."** Anaximenes of Miletus (c. 570–526 B.C.).

## Arguments / reasoning (full detail, all examples from book)
- Familiar with Thales but asks the next question: where does water come from? Answer: **water is condensed air** — we observe water pressed out of air when it rains.
- Press water further → **earth** (cf. earth/sand pressed out of melting ice).
- **Fire is rarefied air.** So air is the origin of earth, water, and fire.
- Earth, air, and fire are all necessary to life, but the underlying substance — the source of all natural change — is air/vapor.

## Key terms
air/vapor as arché; condensation and rarefaction as mechanism — how one substance yields many.

## Significance / legacy
Introduces condensation/rarefaction as an explicit mechanism for how one substance yields many; completes the Milesian trio and sets up the Eleatic problem of how one substance can change into something else.

## Quotes
- "Is there a basic substance that everything else is made of?"; "Can water turn into wine?"
